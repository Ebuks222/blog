---
title: 14 Newborn Babies Killed in Fire at Major Pakistan Hospital
date: 2026-08-26T20:48:00.000+01:00
---
At least 14 newborn babies have died after a fire broke out at the maternity ward of the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences (PIMS) in Islamabad, one of Pakistan’s largest public hospitals.



The fire reportedly started in an air-conditioning unit and spread rapidly after igniting oxygen connections in the ward.



Hospital officials said 15 newborns were in the ward when the fire broke out, with only one infant rescued alive.



The babies were reportedly either in incubators or receiving oxygen support at the time of the incident, according to Pakistan’s Health Minister Mustafa Kamal, who spoke to local media.



A hospital spokesperson said the surviving newborn was rescued by a doctor.



The tragedy has left families devastated, with anguished parents gathering outside the hospital as authorities begin the process of identifying the victims. The bodies are expected to undergo DNA testing to confirm their identities.



One relative, speaking to the BBC, said her three-day-old baby died in the fire. Hivsa Walid, who was at the hospital with her sister-in-law, said they were in a nursery opposite the room where the fire started when they heard a shout at about 6am local time.



Initially, they thought someone had died, but moments later they saw smoke filling the area. They immediately grabbed the newborn, who was so young she had not yet been given a name, and fled the building.



The incident has triggered shock and grief across the hospital. Police have been deployed around the entrance, while fire engines remained at the scene. Broken glass and medical masks were scattered around the premises as families mourned their babies.



Pakistan Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif expressed “deep sorrow and heartfelt regret” over the tragedy and ordered an investigation into the circumstances surrounding the fire.



A fact-finding committee has been given 24 hours to submit its initial report.



The tragedy has also renewed concerns over fire safety and emergency preparedness in Pakistan’s public institutions. The country has experienced several deadly fires in recent years, with poor enforcement of building safety regulations frequently cited as a contributing factor.



PIMS, established in 1985, is a major public-sector healthcare and teaching institution located in central Islamabad. It has a capacity of about 1,200 beds and provides specialist medical and surgical services to patients from Islamabad and surrounding areas, including parts of Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.



The hospital also houses a dedicated children’s hospital providing specialised paediatric care and training for healthcare professionals.



Authorities are continuing investigations into the cause of the fire and the circumstances surrounding the deaths, while grieving families await the identification and release of the remains of their loved ones.
