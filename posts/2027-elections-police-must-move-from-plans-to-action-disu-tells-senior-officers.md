---
title: "2027 Elections: Police Must Move From Plans to Action, Disu Tells Senior
  Officers"
date: 2026-09-23T15:25:00.000+01:00
---
By Vivian Onwuasoanya 



The Sixth Conference and Retreat for Senior Police Officers (CARSPO 2026), holding in Owerri, Imo State, has provided an important opportunity for the Nigeria Police Force to examine the changing nature of security threats and, more importantly, its preparedness for the 2027 General Elections.



The second day of the conference brought together senior police officers for strategic lectures and high-level discussions covering election security, intelligence-led policing, strategic communication, emerging technologies, public demonstrations and police-media relations.



These are not merely conference-room issues. They are matters that will directly affect the safety and confidence of millions of Nigerians as the country moves towards another electoral cycle.



The emphasis placed on early planning and intelligence gathering is particularly significant. Election security cannot be successfully managed through last-minute deployments. It requires credible intelligence, proper risk assessment, effective coordination among commands and agencies, adequate logistics and clear operational responsibilities well before election day.



The Nigeria Police Force therefore has an opportunity to use the lessons and recommendations from CARSPO 2026 to strengthen its preparations across the country.



Equally important is professionalism. The conduct of police officers during elections can have a direct bearing on public confidence in the electoral process. Officers deployed for election duties must understand that their responsibility is to protect lives and property, maintain public order and enforce the law impartially.



The lecture by former Inspector-General of Police, IGP Usman Alkali Baba (Rtd.), on election security governance was therefore timely. As the country approaches 2027, the Force must continue to examine the challenges encountered during previous elections and identify practical measures to address them.



Technology also featured prominently during the conference. Ross Alabo-George's presentation on smart policing and the role of artificial intelligence and digital technologies highlights an increasingly important reality: modern policing cannot depend solely on traditional methods.



From intelligence analysis and information management to crime prevention and operational coordination, technology can provide the Police with tools to respond more effectively to contemporary security challenges. However, technology must be matched with appropriate training, responsible use, institutional safeguards and professional capacity.



The discussion on media relations is equally important.



Reuben Abati's lecture on communication skills for police managers reinforces the need for the Police to communicate effectively with the public. In an era of social media, misinformation and instant information sharing, silence or delayed communication can create uncertainty and sometimes panic.



The Police must therefore continue to strengthen its relationship with credible media organisations while ensuring that accurate, verified and timely information is made available to the public.



For Security Connect, police-media relations are not simply about publicity. They are about public safety. When the public understands what the Police are doing, why certain security measures are being taken and where credible information can be obtained, cooperation becomes easier and misinformation has less room to thrive.



The lecture on policing demonstrations and protests also deserves attention. In a democratic society, the Police have the responsibility to maintain public order while respecting citizens' lawful rights. Professional crowd management, appropriate use of force and effective communication should remain central to the policing of public demonstrations.



Ultimately, the real test of CARSPO 2026 will not be the quality of the speeches delivered or the number of recommendations produced. It will be how effectively those recommendations are translated into action across police commands and formations.



The Nigeria Police Force has a significant responsibility ahead of 2027. The country needs a police institution that is adequately prepared, intelligence-driven, technologically responsive, professionally conducted and capable of communicating effectively with the citizens it serves.



CARSPO 2026 has created the platform for that reflection. The next step must be implementation.



As the conference concludes, the expectation should be clear: strategy must translate into preparedness, preparedness into professional action, and professional action into greater public safety and confidence.



Security Connect believes that a secure electoral process begins long before voters arrive at polling units. It begins with preparation, intelligence, professionalism, coordination and a Police Force that understands the enormous responsibility entrusted to it.
