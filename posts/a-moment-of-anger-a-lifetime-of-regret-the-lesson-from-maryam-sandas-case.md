---
title: "A Moment of Anger, A Lifetime of Regret: The Lesson from Maryam Sanda's Case"
date: 2026-08-01T21:04:00.000+01:00
---
Regardless of where anyone stands on the legal outcome of the Maryam Sanda case, one lesson rises above every argument: anger can destroy lives in a matter of seconds.

What began as a marital dispute reportedly fueled by suspicion, betrayal and intense emotions ended in the loss of a human life. A husband died, children grew up without their father, a family was shattered, and another family watched one of its own spend years behind bars facing the harshest punishment under the law.

No argument, no act of infidelity, no betrayal and no moment of rage is worth a life.

The greatest tragedy is that anger is often temporary, but its consequences can be permanent. Words spoken in anger can damage relationships. Actions taken in anger can destroy families. Violence committed in anger can leave scars that no court judgment can erase.

This case serves as a sobering reminder that emotional intelligence is not a luxury,it is a necessity. Walking away from an argument, seeking help, involving family, counselling, or simply allowing emotions to cool can prevent irreversible tragedies.

Domestic violence is not defined by gender. Whether the victim is a man or a woman, violence in the home must never be excused or justified. Love should never become a reason to harm, and anger should never become a weapon.

The courts will continue to determine guilt and appropriate punishment according to the law. But society must learn the deeper lesson: we must teach emotional control, conflict resolution and peaceful communication before anger turns another disagreement into another obituary.

A few minutes of self-control can save a lifetime of regret.

May we all learn to master our emotions before our emotions master us.
