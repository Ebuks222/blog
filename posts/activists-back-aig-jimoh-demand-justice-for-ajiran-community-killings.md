---
title: Activists Back AIG Jimoh, Demand Justice for Ajiran Community Killings
date: 2026-07-26T11:08:00.000+01:00
---
Civil rights activists have called for justice for the victims of the Ajiran Community killings in Eti-Osa Local Government Area of Lagos State, while condemning what they described as a coordinated campaign of misinformation aimed at discrediting the Nigeria Police Force.

The activists made the call during a solidarity visit to the Assistant Inspector-General of Police, Zone 2 Command, AIG Olohundare Jimoh, commending his commitment to the investigation when he served as Commissioner of Police, Lagos State Command.

According to the group, AIG Jimoh demonstrated professionalism and courage by ensuring a thorough investigation into the killings after assuming office as Commissioner of Police on February 18, 2025. They identified the victims as Mr. Sheriff Ishola Salami, who was killed on April 18, 2023, and Prince Kazeem Ademola Akinloye, the first son of the Ojomu of Ajiranland, who was murdered on August 26, 2024.

The activists stated that the Lagos State Police Command acted on a petition submitted by Olarotimi Williams Chambers, leading to a comprehensive investigation into the two murders. They said the investigation resulted in the arrest of two suspects who allegedly made confessional statements implicating Lagos businessman Hammed Tajudeen Akanbi, popularly known as Ahmed Ajiran, as the alleged sponsor of the killings.

They further disclosed that the suspect was subsequently declared wanted by the police before being arrested in the Republic of Benin and returned to face prosecution.

The group alleged that attempts were made by certain individuals to influence the investigation and prevent prosecution through lobbying of influential personalities and even associates of the then Commissioner of Police. However, they commended the police for remaining steadfast and allowing due process to take its course.

Noting that the matter is currently before a competent court, the activists said they would refrain from making further comments in line with the principle of sub judice. They, however, expressed confidence that the judicial process would ensure justice for the victims and their families.

Describing AIG Jimoh as a dedicated and principled police officer, the activists urged members of the public to disregard what they called false narratives and misinformation being circulated on social media by individuals seeking to undermine the ongoing judicial process.

They reaffirmed their confidence in the Nigeria Police Force and expressed optimism that justice would ultimately prevail for the victims of the Ajiran Community killings.
