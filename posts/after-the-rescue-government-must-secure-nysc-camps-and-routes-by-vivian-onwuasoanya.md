EDITORIAL



The safe rescue of the sixteen National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) members abducted in Kogi State is undoubtedly a moment of relief for their families, the NYSC and the entire nation. We commend the Nigerian Armed Forces, Nigeria Police Force, Department of State Services (DSS), Kogi State Vigilante groups and all others whose courage, intelligence and coordinated efforts made their safe recovery possible.



But beyond the relief and commendation lies a more important question:



What is the government doing to ensure that this does not happen again?



The abduction of Corps Members travelling after completing their Orientation Course should be treated as a serious warning to the Federal Government. It exposes the vulnerability of young Nigerians who have willingly submitted themselves to national service and deserve the full protection of the state.



The NYSC camps must not only be places where young Nigerians receive orientation and training. They must also be secure environments protected by adequate personnel, intelligence and modern security infrastructure.



Government must urgently review the security architecture around all NYSC Orientation Camps across the country, particularly those located in areas facing heightened security challenges.



There should be increased deployment of trained security personnel, proper access control, functional surveillance systems, adequate perimeter fencing, emergency response mechanisms and intelligence-led monitoring around the camps.



Security, however, must not end at the camp gates.



The movement of Corps Members from Orientation Camps to their places of primary assignment must equally receive serious attention. Where security assessments indicate that particular routes are dangerous, government and the NYSC should provide alternative arrangements, security escorts or coordinated transportation for Corps Members.



We cannot continue to wait for an abduction to occur before security measures are intensified.



The Federal Government must move from a reactive approach to a preventive security strategy.



The recent incident should therefore trigger a comprehensive security audit of NYSC camps, accommodation facilities, transportation arrangements and frequently used routes. The findings should result in concrete action, not merely another set of recommendations.



The safety of Corps Members is a national responsibility.



These young Nigerians leave their homes, families and familiar environments to serve in different parts of the country. They are ambassadors of national unity and participants in one of Nigeria's most important nation-building programmes. They should never become easy targets for criminals simply because they are travelling to fulfil their national obligations.



We also commend the families of the abducted Corps Members for their patience during the difficult period and the media for exercising responsibility in reporting the incident without compromising the safety of those in captivity.



But commendation must now give way to accountability and action.



The government must ask difficult questions, identify the security gaps that made the incident possible and close those gaps immediately.



Every NYSC camp should be treated as a strategic national institution requiring adequate protection, not merely as an administrative facility.



The rescued Corps Members have demonstrated remarkable courage and resilience. Their determination to return to their places of primary assignment is a powerful testament to the patriotic spirit of Nigerian youth.



Government must reciprocate that courage by doing everything possible to protect them.



The message from this incident is clear: rescue is good, but prevention is better.



The time has come for the Federal Government to strengthen security around NYSC camps, improve protection along Corps Members' travel routes and invest in intelligence, surveillance and rapid-response capabilities.



The nation cannot afford another abduction before taking action.



Protecting Corps Members is not a favour. It is a duty of the Nigerian state.



Nigeria is ours. Nigeria we serve.
