---
title: "AIG Olohundare Jimoh: Reputation on Trial in the Age of Social Media"
date: 2026-07-26T11:19:00.000+01:00
---
In today's digital age, reputations can be challenged within minutes. Social media has transformed the way information is shared, giving everyone a voice. While this has strengthened public participation, it has also made it easier for allegations to spread long before they are tested against evidence.

This reality underscores the importance of due process, especially when public officials and law enforcement officers are involved.

Assistant Inspector-General of Police Olohundare Jimoh is one of the senior officers whose career has spanned several strategic appointments within the Nigeria Police Force. Throughout his years of service, he has earned recognition for his professionalism, discipline, and commitment to law enforcement. Like every public officer, his actions are open to public scrutiny, but such scrutiny should be guided by facts rather than speculation or trial by social media.

The administration of justice is founded on evidence, fairness, and the rule of law. Allegations, regardless of how widely circulated, remain allegations until they are investigated and determined through the appropriate legal and institutional processes. This principle protects not only public officials but every citizen.

As a former Commissioner of Police in Lagos State and now the Assistant Inspector-General of Police in charge of Zone 2, AIG Jimoh has held positions that demanded difficult decisions and strict adherence to the law. Leadership in policing often attracts criticism, particularly when investigations involve influential individuals or sensitive matters. However, criticism should be distinguished from unverified accusations.

The Nigeria Police Force has established mechanisms for investigating complaints against its personnel. Where credible evidence exists, those mechanisms should be allowed to function independently. Equally, where allegations are false or malicious, those affected deserve the opportunity to defend their reputation through lawful means.

Public confidence in law enforcement depends on accountability, transparency, and fairness. It also depends on respecting the presumption of innocence and resisting the temptation to substitute online narratives for established legal processes.

As conversations continue in the public space, Nigerians should remain committed to responsible civic engagement—demanding accountability where necessary while allowing the law to determine the facts. Upholding these principles strengthens both public institutions and the rule of law.

Ultimately, the reputation of any public servant should be assessed on verifiable evidence, professional conduct, and the outcome of lawful processes—not on unproven claims circulated on social media.
