---
title: Akwa Ibom CP Warns Officers Against Partisanship, Extortion and
  Unprofessional Conduct
date: 2026-08-13T18:15:00.000+01:00
---
As police personnel from Akwa Ibom State prepare to take part in election duties in Osun State, the Commissioner of Police, CP Baba Mohammed Azare, has charged the officers to uphold the highest standards of professionalism, neutrality and discipline throughout the assignment.

The warning came as the officers were being prepared for deployment, with the CP stressing that election security is a sensitive national responsibility that requires strict adherence to the law and the professional ethics of the Nigeria Police Force.

According to a statement issued by the Command’s Public Relations Officer, DSP Timfon John, in Uyo on Thursday, Azare reminded the officers that they would be representing not only the Nigeria Police Force but also the Akwa Ibom State Police Command.

“You are going to Osun State as representatives of the Nigeria Police Force and the Akwa Ibom State Police Command. You must remain completely neutral and professional. Do not allow yourselves to be used by any political party, candidate or individual,” the CP cautioned.

The Commissioner specifically warned the personnel against partisanship, intimidation of voters, extortion, harassment, unnecessary use of force, incivility, abandonment of duty posts and consumption of alcohol while on duty.

He said any conduct capable of damaging the reputation of the Force would not be tolerated, urging the officers to ensure that their actions reflected the integrity, discipline and professionalism expected of police personnel.

Social Media Conduct Under Scrutiny

Beyond their physical conduct at polling locations and other areas of deployment, the officers were also reminded of their responsibilities in the digital space.

Azare drew their attention to the Inspector-General of Police’s Social Media Policy, warning them against posting, livestreaming or circulating sensitive election-related photographs, videos, comments or other materials in ways that could compromise operational security or damage the image of the Nigeria Police Force.

The warning comes at a time when social media has become a major channel for real-time information during elections, making the careless sharing of operational details potentially capable of affecting security arrangements.

The CP therefore urged the officers to exercise restraint and ensure that their online activities remained consistent with established police policies.

No Officer Should Pay for Transportation

Another major issue addressed by the Commissioner was the welfare and transportation arrangements for the deployed personnel.

Azare disclosed that the Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, had made adequate arrangements for the transportation of the officers to Osun State and their eventual return to Akwa Ibom.

He therefore made it clear that no officer was authorised or expected to personally pay transportation costs for the assignment.

The CP also warned commanders and other personnel against collecting money from officers under the guise of transportation or other movement-related expenses.

He directed any officer who is asked to make such payment to immediately report the matter through the appropriate channel.

Protecting the Integrity of the Election

Beyond maintaining order, the officers have been tasked with protecting voters, electoral officials and election materials while ensuring that the electoral process is conducted in a peaceful and secure environment.

Azare directed the personnel to remain under the command and control of their designated commanders, observe the rules of engagement and carry out their responsibilities without fear or favour.

The Commissioner’s message was clear: the police must remain neutral, professional and firmly on the side of the law.

As the officers leave Akwa Ibom for Osun State, their conduct will form part of the broader public perception of the Nigeria Police Force and its role in safeguarding the democratic process.

For CP Azare, the assignment is therefore not merely another deployment. It is a test of discipline, neutrality and professionalism and every officer has a responsibility to ensure that the Force emerges with its integrity intact.
