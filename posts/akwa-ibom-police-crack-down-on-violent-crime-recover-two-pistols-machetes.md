---
title: Akwa Ibom Police Crack Down on Violent Crime, Recover Two Pistols, Machetes
date: 2026-09-23T12:14:00.000+01:00
---
The early hours of September 14, 2026, could have ended differently for a police officer in Ikot Ekweng Village, Ibiono Ibom Local Government Area of Akwa Ibom State.



Inspector Henry was reportedly attacked while on duty, with the assailant allegedly wielding a machete and attempting to dispossess him of his tricycle. The attack left the officer with an injury to his right hand.



But within hours, the Police Command turned the incident into an operational breakthrough, tracing the suspected attacker to his residence and arresting him.



According to the Command’s spokesperson, DSP Timfon John, the incident began at about 5:00 a.m. when police operatives on vehicular patrol received a distress alert from the Command Control Room concerning a violent attack in the area.



The patrol team immediately moved to Ikot Ekweng Village, where they met Inspector Henry and obtained preliminary information about the alleged attack.



Police operatives subsequently launched a follow-up operation that led them to the residence of the suspected assailant, identified as Ini Mfon.



His arrest, however, produced a discovery that has significantly widened the scope of the investigation.



A search of the premises allegedly resulted in the recovery of two locally made pistols and two machetes, all of which have been secured as exhibits.



The discovery of the firearms has shifted the investigation beyond the alleged attack on the police officer. Detectives are now working to establish how the weapons came into the suspect’s possession, whether they may have been used in previous criminal operations and whether the suspect has links to other criminal elements or organised groups operating within or outside Ibiono Ibom.



For the Akwa Ibom State Police Command, the development underscores the importance of rapid response and intelligence-led policing in confronting violent crime and the circulation of illegal firearms.



Commissioner of Police, Akwa Ibom State Command, CP Baba Mohammed Azare, fsi, commended the operatives for what he described as their prompt response, courage and professionalism.



The Commissioner said the arrest and recovery demonstrated the Command’s determination to take the fight against violent crime directly to criminal elements.



He also warned that the Police would sustain intelligence-led operations, targeted raids, aggressive patrols and other lawful security measures aimed at recovering illegal firearms and dismantling criminal networks across the state.



The warning comes against the backdrop of the broader security challenge posed by illegal firearms, which can transform isolated criminal incidents into more serious threats to communities and law enforcement personnel.



Azare further cautioned individuals involved in armed robbery, unlawful possession of firearms and other violent crimes that Akwa Ibom would not provide a safe haven for criminal elements.



Beyond police operations, the Command is also placing emphasis on public cooperation. Residents have been urged to report suspicious persons, suspected criminal hideouts and the possession, movement or circulation of illegal firearms within their communities.



The Police assured members of the public that credible information supplied to the Command would be treated with utmost confidentiality.



For now, Mfon remains in police custody and is assisting investigators. The recovered weapons remain exhibits as detectives work to unravel the wider circumstances surrounding their possession.



The Command said the suspect will be charged to court upon the conclusion of investigations.



What began as an early-morning distress call over an alleged attack on a police officer has therefore opened a much wider criminal investigation—one that could potentially reveal whether the recovered firearms were meant for isolated criminal activity or form part of a broader network operating in the state.
