Police to Publish Findings on Death of 37 Suspected Illegal Miners



By Vivian Onwuasoanya 



The Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, has assured Nigerians that the outcome of the investigation into the deaths of 37 young men detained over alleged illegal mining activities in Niger State will be made public upon the conclusion of the investigation.



The IGP gave the assurance on Tuesday while briefing journalists on the sidelines of the 2026 Conference and Retreat for Senior Police Officers (CARSPO) in Owerri, Imo State, themed, “Developing a Nigeria Police Roadmap for Effective Management of Security During Elections.”



The 37 men were reportedly arrested over suspected illegal mining activities around Borgu, an area of Niger State known for small-scale gold mining. Two days after their arrest, they were found dead in a detention facility operated by the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC).



While the NSCDC attributed the deaths to an apparent disease outbreak, the incident has triggered public concern and unrest, including attacks on public buildings. Authorities subsequently imposed a 24-hour curfew in Minna, the state capital.



Disu said the Police had commenced investigations into the deaths, stressing that detectives were working to establish what actually happened.



\> “We will dwell deeply into the case, but the Police immediate concern is to unravel the mystery behind the death of these 37 young Nigerians. Distinct investigations have commenced. Police detectives are on top of the game. Once the investigation is concluded, we will make it public,” the IGP said.



Responding to questions on whether the NSCDC is constitutionally empowered to maintain detention cells within its facilities, Disu said the immediate priority was to establish the circumstances surrounding the deaths before addressing other issues that may arise from the investigation.



Since assuming office, the IGP has placed emphasis on intelligence-led policing, operational coordination, accountability, technology-driven investigations and professional development within the Nigeria Police Force.



Under his administration, the Force has reported a series of operational outcomes. In April 2026 alone, NPF operations resulted in the arrest of 28 suspected terrorists, 85 suspected kidnappers, 62 armed-robbery suspects, 51 murder and culpable-homicide suspects and 54 suspected cultists. The Force also reported the rescue of 189 kidnapped victims and the recovery of 140 firearms, 1,074 rounds of ammunition and 37 stolen vehicles. 



In May, Disu announced the establishment of the Violent Crime Response Unit (VCRU) as part of efforts to strengthen the Police response to serious and violent crimes. He also disclosed further operational results, including the arrest of suspected terrorists, murder suspects, armed-robbery suspects, kidnappers and cultists, alongside the rescue of 189 kidnapped victims and recovery of firearms, ammunition and stolen vehicles. 



The IGP has also pushed for greater use of technology and data in criminal investigations. The Nigeria Police Force's Crime and Incident Database Centre initiative is designed to improve the capture and management of suspects' information, crime analysis, intelligence gathering, evidence-based investigations and the identification of repeat offenders. 



Another notable operational measure has been the deployment of Deputy Inspectors-General of Police to geopolitical zones, alongside the introduction of coordinated “Handshake Patrols” between neighbouring state commands. The initiative is aimed at closing jurisdictional gaps, improving intelligence sharing and strengthening joint responses to crimes that cross state boundaries. 



On internal reform, Disu has reorganised senior postings across strategic departments and formations of the Force, with the stated objective of strengthening operational efficiency, leadership capacity and service delivery. 



His administration has equally placed emphasis on leadership development and police reform. At a strategic leadership retreat for DIGs and AIGs, the Police Reform Secretariat highlighted reforms including a revised national recruit curriculum and the newly gazetted Nigeria Police Regulations 2025, while discussions focused on community policing, digital transformation, evidence-based budgeting and accountability. 



The operational record has also included major rescue operations. In July 2026, the Force reported the successful conclusion of a 56-day operation in Oyo State that secured the release of seven teachers and 39 pupils who had been held captive for nearly two months. 



Preparing for 2027



The IGP also spoke on preparations by law-enforcement agencies for the forthcoming general elections, revealing that training and retraining would remain a major component of the Police strategy.



He said particular attention would be given to junior officers who have direct contact with voters and communities, with training expected to cover emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence, as well as other aspects of modern election security and policing.



Disu further urged parents, guardians and community leaders to caution young Nigerians against allowing themselves to be used as “cannon fodder” by politicians during the electoral process.



As the country moves towards the 2027 elections, the Police leadership is seeking to combine operational preparedness with intelligence, technology, community engagement and professional conduct.



But beyond the statistics and institutional reforms, the Niger State deaths present another important test for the Force. For the families of the 37 deceased Nigerians, the immediate concern is not simply that an investigation has commenced, but that it establishes clearly what happened, identifies responsibility where evidence supports it, and provides answers that can withstand public scrutiny.



The IGP's commitment to publish the findings therefore places transparency at the centre of the investigation—and the eventual report will be closely watched by the public.
