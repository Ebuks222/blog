---
title: "Beyond Thuggery: Delta Police Challenge Youths to Secure Their Future"
date: 2026-08-20T23:53:00.000+01:00
---
The decision of the Delta State Police Command to begin its International Youth Day 2026 activities with a community engagement at Koka Junction, Asaba, sends an important message about the changing approach to youth policing and crime prevention.



Rather than limiting the commemoration to formal ceremonies, the Command, led by Commissioner of Police CP Yemi Oyeniyi, took the conversation directly to youths, commercial transport operators and members of the public. The message was straightforward: young people must resist being drawn into political violence, criminal activities and the misuse of digital platforms.



The national campaign, “My Future Is Greater Than Political and Digital Thuggery,” captures one of the major challenges facing Nigerian youths today. Across the country, young people are often targeted by politicians, criminal groups and online influencers who seek to exploit their energy, frustration or desire for quick recognition.



Delta State's approach suggests that prevention must begin with direct engagement. By speaking with youths in public spaces and involving transport operators who interact with large numbers of young people daily, the police are extending security awareness beyond the walls of police stations.



The proposition is clear: security agencies must continue to engage youths as partners, not merely as potential offenders. Young people need to understand the consequences of political thuggery, cybercrime and violence, but they must also be offered pathways towards productive citizenship, entrepreneurship and community development.



CP Oyeniyi's emphasis that no criminal or political interest should be allowed to sacrifice the future of young people is particularly relevant. Political contests come and go, but the consequences of violence, criminal records and lost opportunities can remain with a young person for life.



International Youth Day should therefore be more than a date on the calendar. It should serve as a reminder that Nigeria's security future is closely linked to how it guides, educates and empowers its youth population.



The Delta State Police Command's outreach in Asaba provides a practical example of community policing in action—taking the message of peace and responsible citizenship directly to the people.



If sustained beyond the commemorative activities, such engagements can help build trust between the police and young people while reducing the influence of those who seek to exploit them for political or criminal purposes.



Ultimately, the future of Delta State will depend not only on effective law enforcement but also on a generation of youths who choose progress over violence, responsibility over manipulation, and community development over destructive conduct.
