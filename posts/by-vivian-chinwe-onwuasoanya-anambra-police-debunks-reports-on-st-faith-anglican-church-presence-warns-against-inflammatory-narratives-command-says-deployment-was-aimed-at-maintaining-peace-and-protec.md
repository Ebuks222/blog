Command says deployment was aimed at maintaining peace and protecting lives, insists police remain neutral in ecclesiastical dispute.



The Anambra State Police Command has dismissed reports surrounding the presence of police personnel at St. Faith Anglican Church as misleading, urging media organisations and social media users to exercise caution and verify information before publishing reports capable of escalating tensions over the alleged ecclesiastical dispute.



The Police Public Relations Officer (PPRO), SP Tochukwu Ikenga, who made this known in a statement issued in Awka, clarified that police deployments are guided by the need to maintain law and order, prevent a breakdown of peace, protect lives and property, and ensure the safety of all concerned.



The Command stressed that the presence of police personnel at the church should not be interpreted as an endorsement of any party or position in the ecclesiastical disagreement.



According to the statement, the Nigeria Police Force remains a neutral institution committed to enforcing the law, safeguarding citizens’ rights, and preventing disputes from escalating into threats to public peace and security.



The clarification comes amid reports concerning police presence at the Anglican church, with the Command cautioning against narratives that could misrepresent the purpose of security interventions and further heighten tensions within the religious community.



The police also urged media organisations, social media users, and other stakeholders to verify information before publication, exercise restraint in their reporting, and avoid disseminating unconfirmed narratives capable of inflaming tensions or deepening divisions.



The Command emphasised that its security interventions are intended to preserve peace and protect citizens, rather than demonstrate support for any individual or group involved in a dispute.



Reassuring Ndi Anambra of its commitment to public safety, the Command maintained that it would continue to discharge its constitutional responsibilities professionally, impartially, and without fear or favour.



It further warned that no disagreement, whether religious or otherwise, would be allowed to degenerate into a breach of public peace and security in the state.



The Command called on residents to remain calm, cooperate with security agencies, and support efforts to maintain peace and order across Anambra State.
