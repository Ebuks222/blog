COMMUNITY POLICING DELIVERING RESULTS IN APAPA – DPO MOMOH

By Connect News

The community policing initiative introduced by the Commissioner of Police, Lagos State Command, CP Tijani Fatai, is delivering tangible results in Apapa, with improved security and a significant reduction in crime across the division.

Since assuming office as the 42nd Commissioner of Police for Lagos State on March 26, 2026, CP Fatai has made community policing the foundation of his security strategy. During his inaugural address at the Command Headquarters in Ikeja, he pledged to strengthen existing security structures through proactive, intelligence-led and technology-driven policing while promoting stronger collaboration with communities, youth groups, the media and other security agencies.

That vision is now taking shape in Apapa Division under the leadership of the Divisional Police Officer, CSP Kahmaldeen Ayofe Momoh. Once known for container theft, truck-related crimes, cultism and the activities of street gangs, Apapa has witnessed a remarkable improvement in security through sustained community engagement and intelligence-led policing.

In an exclusive interview with Connect News, CSP Momoh explained that the success recorded in the division is the result of close collaboration with traditional rulers, Community Development Associations (CDAs), Community Development Committees (CDCs), youth groups, market leaders, transport unions and other key stakeholders.

According to him, the primary objective of community policing is to reduce crime by building trust between the police and the public.

"Policing cannot succeed without the support of the people. If crime cannot be completely eliminated, it must be reduced to the barest minimum. Through continuous engagement with members of the community, our work has become much easier, and today Apapa is much calmer. The strategy introduced by the Commissioner of Police is producing positive results," he said.

CSP Momoh disclosed that the Lagos State Security Trust Fund has also contributed significantly to the success of the initiative by organising professional training programmes, seminars and capacity-building sessions aimed at improving officers' professionalism and strengthening police-community relations.

He stated that stronger collaboration among the police, other security agencies and residents has greatly improved intelligence gathering and crime prevention.

The DPO revealed that several notorious flashpoints, including Liverpool Road, Waterside, Calabar Quarters (popularly known as Calacuta Street) and the shanties opposite the Nigerian Maritime Administration and Safety Agency (NIMASA), have been cleared of criminal elements through coordinated operations and sustained engagement with community leaders.

"Apapa is now safer for residents, businesses and visitors. Intelligence gathering has improved because members of the public now see the police as partners rather than adversaries," he said.

Looking ahead, CSP Momoh said the division will continue to strengthen community policing through regular town hall meetings, school sensitisation programmes, youth engagement initiatives and joint patrols with recognised vigilance groups.

He identified logistics support, increased funding for community outreach programmes and sustained public trust as critical to maintaining the gains already achieved.

"Policing is not the responsibility of the police alone; it is a collective responsibility. When the police and the people work together, crime reduces. Apapa has demonstrated that community policing works, and with the continued support of CP Tijani Fatai, we will sustain these achievements," he added.

The successes recorded across Apapa's major flashpoints continue to reinforce the effectiveness of the Lagos State Police Command's community policing strategy, positioning the division as a model for modern, community-driven policing in one of Nigeria's busiest commercial and maritime hubs.
