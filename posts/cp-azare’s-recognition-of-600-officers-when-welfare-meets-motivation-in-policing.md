---
title: "CP Azare’s Recognition of 600 Officers: When Welfare Meets Motivation in
  Policing"
date: 2026-08-20T10:49:00.000+01:00
---
The decision by the Commissioner of Police, Akwa Ibom State Command, CP Baba Mohammed Azare, fsi, to recognise 600 officers who participated in the 2026 election duty in Osun State is more than a ceremonial gesture. It is a clear demonstration that welfare in policing goes beyond salaries and allowances—it also includes appreciation, recognition and the assurance that sacrifice and professionalism do not go unnoticed.



The officers were deployed to Osun State to support the Nigeria Police Force in maintaining security during the electoral assignment. Their conduct, according to the Command, reflected discipline, professionalism, impartiality and respect for the rule of law.



Rather than allowing the assignment to end with the officers simply returning to their routine duties, CP Azare chose to publicly acknowledge their performance through Certificates of Commendation. That decision sends an important message across the Command: good service is seen, valued and rewarded.



For police personnel who often work under demanding conditions, such recognition can have a significant impact on morale. Officers are expected to make personal sacrifices, operate under pressure and remain professional even in challenging circumstances. While financial welfare remains important, motivation can also come from knowing that one's dedication is appreciated by leadership.



CP Azare's gesture therefore represents an important dimension of police welfare—the welfare of morale and professional dignity.



By recognising the 600 officers, the Commissioner has not only rewarded past performance but also encouraged future excellence. Recognition creates a culture where officers understand that professionalism is not merely an expectation but a value worthy of appreciation.



The gesture is equally significant because election duties require a high level of discipline and impartiality. Police officers deployed for electoral assignments must navigate politically sensitive environments while maintaining neutrality and protecting citizens, electoral personnel and public infrastructure. Commending officers who successfully discharge such responsibilities reinforces the importance of professionalism in democratic policing.



The response from CSP Soberekon Bamson on behalf of the beneficiaries further underlines the importance of the initiative. The officers viewed the recognition as a source of encouragement and motivation to continue serving with dedication, integrity and discipline.



For the Akwa Ibom State Police Command, the message is clear: leadership is not only about demanding performance; it is also about recognising those who deliver.



At a time when police morale and welfare remain central to conversations about effective policing in Nigeria, initiatives such as this deserve attention. A motivated officer is more likely to approach duty with renewed commitment, while an organisation that consistently recognises excellence can build a stronger culture of accountability and professionalism.



CP Baba Mohammed Azare has therefore demonstrated a leadership approach that connects performance with appreciation and duty with recognition. His commendation of the 600 officers is a reminder that sometimes, a certificate, a word of appreciation and public recognition can go a long way in telling an officer that the sacrifices made in service to the nation matter.



The recognition may have been presented to 600 officers, but its message extends to the entire Akwa Ibom State Police Command: serve professionally, remain disciplined, uphold the law—and excellence will not go unnoticed.
