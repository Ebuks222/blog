The Cross River State Commissioner of Police, CP Ajo Geoffrey Ordue, has called on traditional rulers, community leaders, youths and other stakeholders to strengthen collaboration with the police in tackling crime and maintaining peace across the state.

Ordue made the call during a familiarisation tour of the Ogoja and Ikom Area Commands on October 15 and 16, respectively, where he engaged community stakeholders on security concerns, crime prevention and the need for stronger police-community relations.

During the visit to Ogoja on October 15, the police commissioner met with traditional rulers, civil society organisations, youth groups, members of the Police Community Relations Committee (PCRC), representatives of the Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba communities, market and trade unions, drivers’ associations and other stakeholders.

He urged the groups to support security efforts by sharing credible information with the police, reporting suspicious activities and working with law enforcement officers to address emerging threats.

The engagement continued in Ikom on October 16, where Ordue reiterated the importance of community participation in identifying security challenges and preventing criminal activities.

He stressed that effective policing depends not only on the efforts of security agencies but also on the willingness of residents to cooperate with them.

The commissioner condemned jungle justice, warning residents against taking the law into their own hands whenever someone is suspected of committing a crime.

He urged members of the public to hand suspected offenders over to the police for investigation and allow the appropriate legal process to take its course.

According to him, allegations of criminal conduct do not justify assault, torture or the killing of suspects, adding that every suspect is entitled to due process under the law.

Ordue also called on traditional rulers and community leaders to discourage mob action and promote lawful means of addressing suspected criminal activities.

The police commissioner also cautioned political actors and their supporters against electoral violence, intimidation and the destruction of property, urging them to pursue their political interests within the confines of the law.

He further expressed concern over cultism and drug abuse among youths, calling for collective efforts by families, community leaders and youth organisations to discourage the practices.

He urged young people to shun activities capable of endangering their lives, undermining public safety and threatening the peace of their communities.
