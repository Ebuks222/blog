---
title: CSP Agana Yetunde Champions Community Policing, Stakeholder Partnership
  in Wuye, Abuja
date: 2026-08-30T19:16:00.000+01:00
---
 The deployment of Chief Superintendent of Police (CSP) Agana Yetunde to Wuye Police Division, Abuja, is already attracting attention for her proactive approach to community policing and stakeholder engagement.



With decades of service in the Nigeria Police Force and extensive experience spanning crime prevention, patrol operations, conflict resolution, public relations, disaster and emergency management, gender support, executive security, administration and international peacekeeping, CSP Agana has brought a broad range of professional experience to her new assignment.



Born on January 20, 1976, in Lagos Island and an indigene of Ogun State, Agana began her policing career in the early 1990s. Her early appointments included serving as an Administrative Clerk and Training Officer in Kaduna State Command between 1993 and 1994 and later as a Computer Operations Officer in Kwara State Command from 1994 to 1998.



She subsequently served as a Disaster and Emergency Management Officer in Lagos State Command from 1999 to 2001 and later as Police Public Relations Officer in Kwara State Command between 2001 and 2003.



Her career progressed through a series of operational and leadership responsibilities. Between 2004 and 2008, she served as a Conflict Resolution Officer in Lagos State Command and later as a Divisional Crime Officer from 2008 to 2010.



From 2010 to 2015, she served as Commanding Officer for Patrol and Guard Operations in Lagos State Command, where she was responsible for coordinating personnel and supporting security operations.



Her experience also extends to executive security. Between 2015 and 2019, she served as Chief Security Officer to the Deputy Governor of Lagos State, an assignment that further strengthened her experience in executive protection and high-level security coordination.



Between 2019 and 2021, she served as Family Support Officer in the Gender Unit of Kaduna State Command, a role that exposed her to issues requiring sensitivity, conflict management and attention to the welfare of vulnerable members of society.



At Force Headquarters, Abuja, she served as an Administration and Human Resource Officer between 2021 and 2022.



Her professional experience also includes international peacekeeping assignments. She served with the United Nations Mission in Darfur (UNAMID) between 2010 and 2011 and later served as a United Nations Police Adviser with the United Nations Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) from October 2023 to October 2024.



The international assignments exposed her to peacekeeping operations, humanitarian interventions, conflict resolution, gender perspectives, logistics, terrorism studies and international peace and security practices.



In November 2024, CSP Agana assumed the position of Chief Superintendent of Police in the Administration Department at Force Headquarters, Abuja, where she served until November 2025. She was subsequently posted as Divisional Police Officer, bringing her operational and administrative experience to divisional policing.



Beyond her professional credentials, CSP Agana's approach to policing at Wuye has been characterised by early engagement with stakeholders and a deliberate effort to strengthen the relationship between the police and residents.



Since assuming responsibility at the division, she has reached out to the Police Community Relations Committee (PCRC), religious institutions, traditional leaders, transport operators, market executives and other community stakeholders.



Among the groups engaged are the Wuye PCRC, churches, traditional heads, Okada Riders Association, Keke Marwa executives and Wuye Ultra Modern Market executives.



The engagements are aimed at encouraging residents and community leaders to support the police through information sharing, cooperation and collective efforts toward crime prevention.



The approach reflects the growing importance of intelligence-led and community-centred policing, where residents and other stakeholders are regarded as critical partners in identifying security concerns and preventing crime.



For CSP Agana, community engagement is not separate from police work but an important component of effective crime prevention. By establishing communication channels with different groups within the division, the police can better understand emerging security concerns and respond to them more effectively.



Her professional record also includes commendations and awards for crime prevention from the Kwara State Police Command. She has equally been recognised as an Ambassador for Peace by the Universal Peace Federation.



Her international service earned her United Nations medals, while her professional development has included United Nations certifications in areas such as peacekeeping, logistics, humanitarian law, conflict resolution, gender perspectives, terrorism studies and peace operations.



She is also associated with International Women Police and the International Association of Chiefs of Police.



Academically, CSP Agana holds an Ordinary National Diploma and Higher National Diploma in Hotel and Catering Management from Kwara State Polytechnic. She also obtained her secondary education certificates from Agbeyangi Secondary School and Mosan Grammar School in Lagos, as well as her First School Leaving Certificate from Abesan Primary School, Lagos.



Interestingly, her background in hotel and catering management also provided her with seven years of practical experience in service delivery and organisational operations before and alongside her broader professional development.



With Wuye being a dynamic urban community with a diverse population and commercial activities, her combination of operational policing, administration, public relations, conflict resolution and international experience could provide a useful foundation for strengthening security cooperation within the division.



Her early engagement with community groups indicates a leadership style that places visibility, communication, trust and partnership at the centre of policing.



For residents of Wuye, the effectiveness of this approach will ultimately be measured by its impact on crime prevention, intelligence gathering, public confidence and the overall security environment within the division.



As CSP Agana settles into her assignment, her immediate priority appears to be building a stronger partnership between the police and the community. Her message to stakeholders is straightforward: security is a shared responsibility, and sustainable peace requires the cooperation of everyone.



With more than three decades of police service, extensive operational and administrative experience, executive security exposure and international peacekeeping credentials, CSP Agana Yetunde brings a wealth of experience to her assignment.



Her tenure at Wuye is therefore being watched as another opportunity for a seasoned police professional to translate years of experience into practical community policing, stronger stakeholder collaboration and improved public safety.
