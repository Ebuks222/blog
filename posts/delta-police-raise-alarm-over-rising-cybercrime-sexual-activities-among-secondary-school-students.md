---
title: Delta Police Raise Alarm Over Rising Cybercrime, Sexual Activities Among
  Secondary School Students.
date: 2026-08-26T21:05:00.000+01:00
---
The Divisional Police Officer (DPO) of Ugborikoko Police Division in Uvwie Local Government Area of Delta State, CSP Omosetemi Agbede Zuokumor, has raised alarm over the growing involvement of secondary school students in cybercrime and other risky activities.



CSP Zuokumor expressed concern over the increasing exposure of teenagers to criminal activities, particularly online fraud, as well as reports of sexual activity among some secondary school students.



The DPO made the disclosure while highlighting the need for parents, schools, government authorities and security agencies to intensify efforts to protect young people from influences capable of derailing their education and future.



According to her, the situation has become particularly worrying as some students are allegedly being introduced to cybercrime at a very young age.



She further alleged that, in one of the cases encountered by the police, a male secondary school student was found in possession of more than 85 Australian phone numbers, which were reportedly linked to suspected online fraud activities.



The DPO also disclosed that the police recovered what she described as a basketful of mobile phones, including iPhone 16 and iPhone 17 models, as well as Samsung phones, in connection with investigations into suspected cybercrime activities.



The revelations have raised fresh concerns about the growing accessibility of sophisticated mobile devices and digital platforms to young people and how such technology may be exploited for criminal purposes.



CSP Zuokumor also expressed concern over reports of sexual activity among some secondary school students, warning that early exposure to risky behaviours could have serious consequences for their education, personal development and overall wellbeing.



She stressed the importance of early intervention, noting that parents and guardians have a critical role to play in monitoring their children's activities, friendships and use of smartphones and social media platforms.



The development has also renewed calls for greater collaboration between the police, schools, parents, the Delta State Government and the Ministry of Education to address the factors exposing young people to cybercrime and other risky behaviours.



Stakeholders say the response should not be limited to punishment but should also include sustained counselling, digital-safety education, mentorship and closer parental supervision.



Schools have equally been encouraged to strengthen guidance and counselling programmes and establish safe channels through which students can report peer pressure, exploitation, abuse or attempts to recruit them into criminal activities.



Parents, on their part, are being urged to take a more active interest in their children's digital lives, including the applications they use, the people they communicate with online and the type of content they consume.



The police warning comes amid broader concerns about the impact of social media, peer pressure and easy access to digital technology on young people.



CSP Zuokumor emphasised that allowing teenagers to become deeply involved in criminal activities at an early age could have long-term consequences for both the individuals and society.



She therefore called for a coordinated approach involving law enforcement agencies, educational institutions, parents, community leaders and government authorities to prevent young people from being drawn into cybercrime.



The concerns over sexual activity among secondary school students have similarly highlighted the need for age-appropriate education, counselling and stronger parental guidance to help teenagers make responsible decisions and protect themselves from exploitation and abuse.



For stakeholders, the priority should be early identification of vulnerable students, rehabilitation where necessary and prevention of further recruitment into criminal networks.



The situation in Ugborikoko and the wider Uvwie area serves as a reminder of the growing challenges confronting young people in the digital age and the urgent need for responsible use of technology.



CSP Omosetemi Agbede Zuokumor urged parents and other stakeholders not to ignore warning signs, stressing that protecting young people from cybercrime and other harmful influences requires collective responsibility.



She reiterated the commitment of the police to investigating suspected criminal activities and taking appropriate action in accordance with the law, while encouraging members of the public to provide useful information that could assist security agencies in preventing crime and protecting vulnerable young people.
