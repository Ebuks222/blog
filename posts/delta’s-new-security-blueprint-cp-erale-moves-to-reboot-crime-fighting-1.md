New Commissioner promises intelligence-led policing, stronger community partnerships, inter-agency synergy and zero tolerance for corruption



By Vivian Chinwe O.



The arrival of CP Samuel Etaifo Erale, psc, mnips, as the 25th Commissioner of Police, Delta State Command, signals a fresh phase in the State’s security architecture, with the new police chief unveiling an ambitious agenda anchored on intelligence-led policing, community partnership, operational discipline and a decisive response to emerging criminal threats.



Taking charge of the Command at a time when security challenges are becoming increasingly sophisticated, CP Erale has made it clear that his administration will not be built around routine policing alone. His strategy is to rethink, restructure and reboot the Command’s approach to crime fighting.



At his inaugural press briefing, the Commissioner identified the protection of lives and property as the central responsibility of the Police Command, assuring residents and visitors that the Force would deploy every lawful means necessary to make Delta safer.



He acknowledged the strategic importance of Delta State to Nigeria’s economy and particularly to the Niger Delta region, stressing that the State’s economic potential and stability are inseparable from the effectiveness of its security architecture.



FROM REACTIVE TO PROACTIVE POLICING



One of the most significant elements of CP Erale’s agenda is his determination to move the Command further away from reactive policing.



Under the new strategy, intelligence gathering, data analysis, technology and continuous assessment of crime patterns will become increasingly central to operational decision-making.



The Commissioner intends to review existing strategies across the Command to establish what is working, what requires improvement and what must be fundamentally changed.



This review will extend to tactical teams, whose activities and operations are expected to be restructured around measurable performance indicators.



The objective is clear: identify threats before they mature into crimes, deploy resources where they are most needed and make policing more preventive than merely responsive.



NO ROOM FOR CORRUPTION OR ETHICAL MISCONDUCT



For CP Erale, effective policing begins with the Police themselves.



His administration has placed discipline and professionalism at the forefront of its agenda, with officers expected to operate within Police Rules and Regulations, Public Service Rules and the broader framework of the rule of law.



The Commissioner declared zero tolerance for corruption, extortion and ethical misconduct, while emphasising respect for human rights and professional conduct.



But his message is not solely punitive.



Officers who distinguish themselves through integrity, courage and exceptional service, he said, will be recognised and rewarded.



The approach suggests an attempt to create a Command where accountability and professionalism are matched with recognition for officers who uphold the values of the Force.



COMMUNITIES AS THE FIRST LINE OF INTELLIGENCE



CP Erale’s security blueprint also places communities at the centre of crime prevention.



He plans to deepen engagement with traditional rulers, community leaders, youth organisations, women’s groups, religious bodies and other stakeholders.



The strategy recognises a reality that has become increasingly important in modern policing: the Police cannot effectively secure every community without the cooperation of the people living there.



Community-based intelligence, early warning and timely reporting of suspicious activities will therefore be encouraged.



The Commissioner wants residents to see the Police not simply as an institution that arrives after a crime has occurred, but as a security partner that can work with communities to prevent criminal activities in the first place.



UNITING THE SECURITY ARCHITECTURE



Another critical component of the new administration is stronger synergy with sister security agencies.



CP Erale has pledged to promote joint operations, intelligence sharing, coordinated patrols and rapid responses to emerging threats.



This is particularly significant in dealing with crimes that transcend traditional jurisdictional boundaries, including kidnapping, cultism, violent crime, oil-related criminality and other organised criminal activities.



A coordinated security response, rather than isolated operations by individual agencies, could provide the State with a more integrated mechanism for detecting and neutralising threats.



A HARDER FRONT AGAINST CRIMINAL NETWORKS



The Commissioner has also signalled an intensified operational campaign against some of Delta’s most pressing security threats.



Kidnapping, cultism, armed robbery, cybercrime, violent crime and oil-related crimes have all been identified as areas requiring sustained attention.



Rather than adopting a one-size-fits-all approach, CP Erale intends to deploy personnel and resources according to crime trends and identified hotspots.



This means that intelligence will increasingly determine where personnel are deployed, how operations are structured and where security pressure is concentrated.



RESTORING THE POLICE–PUBLIC TRUST



Beyond arrests and tactical operations, CP Erale recognises that public confidence remains one of the most important weapons available to the Police.



His administration intends to strengthen police visibility, improve responsiveness to complaints and ensure that citizens are treated with dignity and respect regardless of status.



The Commissioner’s broader vision is to establish a policing culture where residents regard the Police as partners in security.



That relationship, however, will require more than public statements. It will depend on the everyday experience of citizens when they encounter police officers, report crimes or seek protection.



BUILDING ON THE FOUNDATION



While announcing his agenda, CP Erale was careful to acknowledge the contributions of his predecessor, CP Yemi John Oyeniyi, psc, acipm, mnips, and the Command’s management team.



He pledged to consolidate the gains already recorded rather than discard them, declaring that his administration would “build forward, not backwards.”



It is a philosophy that places continuity alongside reform—retaining what works while introducing new approaches to areas that demand change.



A SECURITY PARTNERSHIP WITH THE PEOPLE



Perhaps the most important message from the new Commissioner was his appeal to residents to become active participants in the security process.



His call—“If you see something, say something”—captures the central philosophy of his community policing agenda.



For Delta State, the coming months will therefore be a test of implementation.



The blueprint is ambitious: disciplined policing, intelligence-driven operations, stronger tactical teams, technology-enabled crime prevention, community intelligence, inter-agency cooperation and improved public confidence.



For CP Samuel Etaifo Erale, the task now is to translate that blueprint into measurable security outcomes.



As he begins his tenure as the 25th Commissioner of Police in Delta State, the expectation is not simply that the Command will respond to crime, but that it will anticipate threats, disrupt criminal networks and build a security system in which the people of Delta State become active partners in protecting their communities.



The message from the new police leadership is unmistakable: Delta’s crime-fighting strategy is being rebooted—and the real measure of the new era will be in the results.
