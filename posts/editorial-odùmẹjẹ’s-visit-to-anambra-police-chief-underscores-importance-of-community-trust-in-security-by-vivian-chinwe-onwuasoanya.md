  Importance of Community Trust in Security  By Vivian Chinwe Onwuasoanya"

Public cooperation, religious engagement and accountable policing must remain central to efforts to sustain peace and security in Anambra State.



Security is not achieved through enforcement alone. While the Police have a constitutional responsibility to protect lives and property, lasting security also depends on public trust, community cooperation and the willingness of residents to work with law enforcement agencies.



This is why the recent engagement between the Anambra State Police Command and the Spiritual Director of the Mountain of Holy Ghost Intervention and Deliverance Ministry, Odùmẹjẹ, deserves attention.



On Wednesday, October 21, 2026, the Commissioner of Police, Anambra State Command, CP Nnanna Oji Ama, psc, mnips, received the cleric during a courtesy visit to the Command Headquarters in Awka. The meeting provided an opportunity to reinforce the importance of collaboration between the Police and religious institutions in promoting peace, public safety and harmonious coexistence.



In a statement issued by the Police Public Relations Officer, SP Tochukwu Ikenga, the Command reaffirmed its commitment to sustained engagement with religious leaders, traditional institutions, community stakeholders and other critical partners.



The message is significant because security challenges are rarely resolved by law enforcement efforts in isolation. Effective policing requires cooperation from the people, access to credible information and a relationship in which citizens feel confident enough to report suspicious activities and communicate their concerns without fear.



Religious leaders occupy an influential position in many Nigerian communities. Their congregations often extend across different social groups, giving them opportunities to promote peaceful coexistence, discourage violence, encourage responsible conduct and support conflict resolution.



When properly harnessed, this influence can complement police efforts, particularly in communities where dialogue and trusted local voices can help prevent tensions from escalating into security threats.



However, community engagement must go beyond courtesy visits, photographs and official statements. Its real value lies in the practical outcomes it produces.



For the Anambra State Police Command, this means sustaining regular consultations with religious and traditional leaders, responding to legitimate community concerns and ensuring that information provided by residents receives appropriate attention.



It also means demonstrating professionalism, fairness and accountability in the discharge of police duties. Public trust cannot be secured through appeals alone; it must be earned through conduct, transparency and consistent respect for the rights of citizens.



Equally important is the responsibility of the public. Residents must recognise that security is a shared responsibility and that credible information can help security agencies prevent crime before it occurs. Religious institutions and community leaders can assist by encouraging lawful conduct, discouraging the spread of unverified information and directing security concerns to the appropriate authorities.



CP Nnanna's emphasis on people-oriented policing and stakeholder collaboration therefore reflects an important principle: the relationship between the Police and the public should not be defined solely by arrests, investigations and enforcement, but also by dialogue, cooperation and mutual confidence.



Nevertheless, the effectiveness of this approach will ultimately be measured by its impact on the safety of communities, the responsiveness of the Police and the level of confidence residents have in law enforcement.



The visit by Odùmẹjẹ presents an opportunity to strengthen this relationship. The challenge before the Command is to translate the goodwill generated by such engagements into sustained partnerships that produce tangible security benefits.



As Anambra State continues to pursue peace and stability, the Police must remain accessible to the communities they serve, while residents and influential institutions must play their part in supporting lawful and constructive security initiatives.



Ultimately, effective policing is not simply about maintaining order; it is about building a relationship in which the Police and the public work together towards a common goal. When trust is strengthened, information flows more freely, cooperation improves and the foundations for lasting security become stronger.



The Anambra State Police Command must therefore continue to invest in community relations, not as a ceremonial obligation, but as an essential pillar of effective policing.
