---
title: EFCC Chairman Olukoyede Warns Girls Against Sextortion, Cybercrime
date: 2026-10-17T19:31:00.000+01:00
---
By Vivian Onwuasoanya



The Executive Chairman of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), Ola Olukoyede, has warned young Nigerian girls against sextortion, online blackmail and cybercrime, urging them to protect their integrity, embrace lawful means of earning money and become responsible citizens.



Olukoyede gave the warning on Monday, October 12, 2026, in Abuja, during an event organised by the African Girl Guild (AGG) to mark the International Day of the Girl Child, with the theme, “Invest in Her, Empower Her and Let Her Lead.”



Represented by the Head of the EFCC’s Enlightenment and Re-orientation Unit, Assistant Commander of the EFCC, ACE I Aisha Muhammed, the EFCC chairman expressed concern over the growing incidence of sextortion in the country, noting that perpetrators exploit the vulnerabilities of young women to extort money from them.



He urged girls to exercise caution in their relationships and avoid falling victim to criminals who use intimate images and other sensitive information to threaten and blackmail their victims.



“Girls need to be careful about relationships and not fall into the antics of criminals and sextortionists. Integrity in a relationship will safeguard you against such villains. A girl with integrity will always outlast a girl with only make-up. Your integrity is your crown. Do not sell it cheap,” he said.



Olukoyede also cautioned young girls against participating in cybercrime, particularly serving as account mules for internet fraudsters or engaging in online blackmail.



“If you engage in cybercrime; if you become an account mule for Yahoo boys; if you blackmail people online, you are not just committing a crime, you are destroying Nigeria's image, the same Nigeria you will need for a lot of things,” he warned.



The EFCC chairman stressed that the girl child must understand her responsibility in building a society founded on integrity, accountability and respect for the law.



According to him, young girls should report wrongdoing, volunteer in their communities and recognise themselves as important stakeholders in the development of the country.



He further encouraged them to acquire practical and digital skills, including coding, tailoring, baking and content creation, as alternatives to criminal activities and pathways to financial independence.



“Not everything you earn is for squandering. Save at least a certain percentage like 20 per cent, know your needs and wants. Financial awareness is self-defence. Don't let anyone use your account to commit fraud. That account in your name can take you to prison,” he said.



Olukoyede maintained that financial independence would empower girls to make informed decisions, resist negative influences and protect themselves from exploitation.



“An empowered girl is not waiting for someone to rescue her, she is educated; she is skilled; financially independent and because she is independent, nobody can buy her conscience,” he said.



He disclosed that the EFCC had established Integrity Clubs and Zero Tolerance Clubs in schools across Nigeria to promote ethical values among young people, urging the participants to become ambassadors of integrity in their schools and communities.



Speaking at the event, Denen Petronella, Executive Assistant to Jindu Peter, founder of the African Girl Guide, said investing in the girl child goes beyond education to include confidence-building, skills development, protection, mentorship and access to opportunities.



Petronella explained that girls should not only be prepared for the future but also provided with the necessary support and opportunities to shape it.



“At African Girl Guide, we believe a girl should not only be prepared for her future; she should be given the confidence, support and opportunities to help shape it. She learns; she leads; she thrives,” she said.



She added that empowering girls requires deliberate efforts to establish support systems that enable them to discover their potential, develop leadership abilities and achieve their aspirations.



As part of its commitment to advancing girl-child empowerment, the African Girl Guild announced the launch of the AGG Camp Amani School Club, designed to provide girls with sustained opportunities for learning and personal development throughout the school year.



According to Petronella, the initiative will help girls build leadership and life skills, strengthen their confidence and explore opportunities that will prepare them for future responsibilities.



The event was attended by representatives of government ministries, departments and agencies, educational institutions, as well as students from various secondary schools in Abuja.



The programme highlighted the importance of protecting girls from online exploitation, promoting integrity and investing in their education and skills as essential steps towards building a safer and more empowered generation.
