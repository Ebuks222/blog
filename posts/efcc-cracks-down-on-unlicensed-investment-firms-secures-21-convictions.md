---
title: EFCC Cracks Down on Unlicensed Investment Firms, Secures 21 Convictions
date: 2026-09-29T20:14:00.000+01:00
---
The conviction of 21 companies by the Federal High Court in Lafia, Nasarawa State, for allegedly operating financial investment businesses without the required licence from the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is a significant development in Nigeria’s ongoing fight against investment fraud and financial crimes.



Justice Anyalewa Onoja-Alapa convicted and sentenced the companies following their prosecution by the Abuja Zonal Directorate of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC). The companies were arraigned on September 15 and 16, 2026, on one-count charges relating to the operation of specialised financial businesses without valid licences.



Among the companies convicted were Ngwuoke Daniels Technologies, Credio Banco Ltd, Digital Company Ltd, Co Request Capital Nigeria Ltd, Mega Drop Quality Stores Ltd, Norland Global Ltd, Oxford International, Creative Agriculture Cooperative, Qnet Nigeria Ltd, Qnet Professional Skill Academy Ltd and Mastermind Energy & Agro Nigeria Ltd.



Others included Atus West Africa Investment Company, Eatrich360 Farms, Matag Agro General Services, Viables X Agribusiness Ltd, Kwakol Markets Ltd, Light Shade International Ltd, Value Growth Ltd, B12 Synergy Nigeria Ltd, Phresh Farm Ltd and Omega Pro Global Resources.



The cases centred on allegations that the companies engaged in financial investment management and related activities without valid licences from the SEC, contrary to Section 57(1) of the Banks and Other Financial Institutions Act, 2020.



According to the EFCC, its prosecution was supported by intelligence reports, statements from investigating officers, letters relating to investigation activities, as well as responses obtained from the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) and the SEC.



Following the presentation of the prosecution's evidence, the court convicted the companies and imposed a fine of N30 million on each company. The court also ordered the payment of N200,000 for each day the offence was committed.



The case highlights an important security and economic concern: the vulnerability of Nigerians to unregulated investment schemes and financial operators who may present attractive opportunities without the necessary regulatory approval.



For the public, the development reinforces the need for greater caution before committing hard-earned money to investment platforms, companies or schemes. Registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission alone should not automatically be interpreted as authorisation to provide regulated financial or investment services. Prospective investors must also establish whether the relevant operator has the required approval from the appropriate regulatory authority.



The EFCC stated that the companies came under investigation following actionable intelligence linking them to investment fraud and unlicensed operations. The Commission further said its investigators had invited the promoters for interrogation in December 2022 and January 2023, but the invitations were allegedly ignored.



The prosecution therefore also sends a message about the importance of cooperation with lawful investigations. Where financial intelligence raises questions about a company's activities, regulatory compliance and financial operations, timely engagement with investigators and regulators is essential.



Beyond the convictions themselves, the development raises a broader question about consumer protection in Nigeria's increasingly digital financial environment. As investment opportunities move online and social media becomes a major avenue for advertising financial products, members of the public can easily be exposed to schemes whose regulatory status they may not understand.



This is where stronger public awareness, effective regulatory oversight and responsible financial journalism become important. Nigerians need accessible information on how to distinguish licensed investment operators from unlicensed entities, while regulators must continue to strengthen monitoring and enforcement.



The EFCC's action also demonstrates the importance of collaboration among institutions such as the EFCC, SEC and CAC in identifying suspicious financial activities and protecting the public from potential investment-related losses.



For Security Connect Media, the conviction of these 21 companies should be viewed not simply as a courtroom development, but as part of the wider national security conversation. Financial crimes can have serious consequences for individuals, families, businesses and the wider economy. Protecting citizens from fraudulent or unregulated financial schemes is therefore an important component of economic security.



The public should remain vigilant, conduct proper checks before investing, and report suspicious investment activities to the appropriate authorities.



The details of the prosecution and convictions were contained in a statement issued by Dele Oyewale, Head, Media & Publicity, Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC).
