---
title: EFCC Dismisses Over 40 Staff for Corruption, Says Olukoyede
date: 2026-09-01T23:57:00.000+01:00
---
The Chairman of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), Mr Ola Olukoyede, has disclosed that more than 40 personnel of the commission have been dismissed for corruption and financial malpractice in the past three years.



Olukoyede made the disclosure on Monday in Abuja during a media interactive session marking his three-year stewardship at the commission.



According to him, more than five of the affected personnel are currently facing prosecution, while case files involving others are being prepared for prosecution.



«“In the past two and a half to three years of my service, I have asked them to dismiss over 40 staff on account of corruption and financial malpractice. More than five of them are being prosecuted at the moment,” he said.»



The EFCC chairman explained that the disciplinary measures were part of efforts to ensure that commission personnel were held to the same standards of accountability expected of other Nigerians.



“You can follow those cases in court; they are public knowledge,” he said.



Olukoyede stressed that EFCC officers must maintain clean records while investigating and prosecuting financial crimes.



“You must be sure that your hands are clean. You can’t be fighting corruption when your hands are soiled with corrupt practices,” he said.



As part of the commission’s internal reform efforts, Olukoyede also announced the renaming of the former Internal Affairs Department to the Department of Ethics and Integrity.



He said the department would strengthen the commission’s internal cleansing efforts, including monitoring staff conduct, gifts and sources of income.



Under the new policy, personnel will be required to declare gifts above a specified value, including gifts received from friends and relatives both within and outside Nigeria.



The commission will also define the categories of gifts officers may accept and require personnel to account for their sources of income and standard of living.



Olukoyede further called for stronger institutional reforms and policy changes to tackle corruption, noting that law enforcement alone could not win the fight against financial crime.



«“The most effective system that fights financial crime is not law enforcement. It is the policy regime, institutional reforms that close leakages,” he said.»
