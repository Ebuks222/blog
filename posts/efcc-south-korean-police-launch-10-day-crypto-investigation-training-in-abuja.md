---
title: EFCC, South Korean Police Launch 10-Day Crypto Investigation Training in Abuja
date: 2026-10-17T19:38:00.000+01:00
---
By Vivian Onwuasoanya



The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), in partnership with the South Korean Police, has launched a 10-day specialised training programme on asset tracing and cryptocurrency investigations to strengthen Nigeria’s capacity to combat sophisticated financial crimes and recover illicit proceeds.



The training, which commenced on October 13, 2026, at the EFCC Academy in Karu, Abuja, brings together more than 30 EFCC operatives for intensive instruction in digital financial investigations, cryptocurrency tracing and asset recovery.



Speaking at the opening ceremony, EFCC Executive Chairman, Ola Olukoyede, represented by the Acting Commandant of the EFCC Academy, Assistant Commander of the EFCC, ACE I Joseph Ogwiji, stressed the need for investigators to continually upgrade their skills in response to the growing use of digital financial services and virtual assets.



He noted that while these technologies support legitimate economic activities, they are increasingly being exploited to facilitate fraud, transfer criminal proceeds and conceal the identities of those involved.



“Our response must be informed, technically competent and grounded in lawful investigative procedures,” he said.



Olukoyede explained that the growing sophistication of cross-border crimes and money laundering had made it essential for investigators to understand transaction records, track funds across multiple platforms and link digital evidence to individuals under investigation.



He added that such capabilities were critical to tackling economic and financial crimes in an increasingly interconnected global financial system.



In his remarks, the Consul and Police Attaché at the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Nigeria, Eun-pyo Hong, described the programme as a significant milestone in law enforcement cooperation between both countries.



He disclosed that the training was the first of its kind to be conducted by the Korean Government in Africa, marking the first anniversary of a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) signed between the EFCC and the Korean National Police Agency (KNPA) in Seoul, South Korea, in October 2025.



“I am especially pleased that our first programme in Africa is taking place here in Nigeria, a leading country in West Africa. And I am also very pleased to start this programme with the EFCC, one of Nigeria’s most respected law-enforcement institutions,” Hong said.



The MoU, signed by Olukoyede and the Deputy Commissioner General of the KNPA, Park Seong-Ju, provides a framework for cooperation in information exchange, capacity building, institutional strengthening, asset recovery coordination, research collaboration, public education and training exchanges, among other areas.



Leading the South Korean delegation, Woosung Kwon of the Korean Police Investigation Academy, alongside Inspector Kim Sung Jae and Sergeant Jung Youngmo, expressed appreciation to the EFCC management and organising team for their hospitality and cooperation.



Kwon observed that financial crimes were evolving rapidly, with criminals increasingly exploiting cryptocurrency, cross-chain tools and the dark web to conceal illicit funds.



“Crypto crime has no borders, so our cooperation must have no borders, too,” he said, emphasising the importance of international collaboration in addressing emerging financial threats.



He explained that the training would feature practical sessions on open-source investigation techniques and cryptocurrency tracing, taking participants through the process of identifying initial leads, tracking digital assets, securing assets and preparing evidence for court proceedings.



Also speaking, the Head of External Cooperation at the EFCC, Aliyu Wali, represented by Assistant Commander of the EFCC, ACE II Michael Kayang, described the programme as timely, given the increasing exploitation of virtual assets and emerging technologies for financial crimes.



He said strengthening investigators’ ability to trace, analyse and investigate virtual asset transactions had become imperative in the fight against cyber-enabled financial crimes.



Wali urged participants to maximise the opportunity by actively engaging with their South Korean counterparts and applying the knowledge gained to their operational responsibilities.



The 10-day programme underscores the growing importance of international law enforcement partnerships, technical expertise and digital evidence in tackling cryptocurrency-related crimes, tracing illicit wealth and strengthening asset recovery efforts.



It is also expected to deepen operational cooperation between Nigeria and South Korea as both countries work towards addressing the evolving challenges posed by transnational financial crimes.
