---
title: EFCC Trains South-East Non-Profits on Terrorism Financing Risks
date: 2026-09-10T22:47:00.000+01:00
---
The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), in collaboration with Spaces for Change, has trained Non-Profit Organisations (NPOs) operating in the South-East on how to identify, prevent and mitigate vulnerabilities that could expose them to terrorist financing.



The training formed part of Nigeria’s ongoing second national assessment of terrorism-financing risks within the non-profit sector, aimed at strengthening safeguards without disrupting legitimate humanitarian and development activities.



Speaking during the exercise, Executive Director of Spaces for Change, Victoria Ibezim-Ohaeri, stressed that the assessment was not intended to stigmatise NPOs or portray them as channels for terrorism financing.



According to her, the exercise was designed to help organisations understand the risks within their operating environment and put appropriate measures in place to protect their institutions, funds and beneficiaries.



She said, “The assessment would help organisations understand the risks around them and take appropriate steps to protect themselves. The essence of the programme is not to scare NPOs; it is to help us understand the terrorism-financing risk assessment process, the national risk assessment and the risks that may exist within the NPO sector.”



Ibezim-Ohaeri explained that consultations were being conducted across different parts of the country because the risks and operational realities confronting NPOs vary from one region to another.



She noted that information obtained from organisations working in different locations would help authorities develop a more accurate understanding of the sector and identify areas requiring stronger safeguards.



She further warned that terrorist actors could exploit institutional weaknesses by creating entities that appear legitimate or taking advantage of vulnerabilities in genuine humanitarian organisations to move funds, access resources or facilitate terrorist activities.



The ongoing assessment, which commenced in March 2026, is Nigeria’s second national review of terrorism-financing risks within the NPO sector. It follows an earlier assessment conducted in 2021 and published in 2022.



The current exercise is expected to examine the funding patterns, organisational structures, field operations, due-diligence procedures and existing safeguards of non-profit organisations, while identifying gaps that could be exploited by criminals or terrorist actors.



The assessment team comprises relevant government agencies and stakeholders involved in Nigeria’s anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing framework, including the EFCC, Special Control Unit Against Money Laundering (SCUML) and the Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit (NFIU).



Leading the assessment team, Korede Abdul-Aziz urged participants to engage openly with the process and provide accurate information about the realities, challenges and vulnerabilities affecting their organisations.



Also speaking, an Assistant Director at the Federal Ministry of Budget and Economic Planning, Fidelis Akpoagu, urged NPOs to ensure that their organisations are properly registered and operate in compliance with relevant regulatory requirements.



Akpoagu also called for stronger coordination among non-profit organisations, particularly through functional networks that would promote information sharing and help expose individuals or groups falsely presenting themselves as legitimate humanitarian or development organisations.



He disclosed that the ministry was considering improved data systems for the sector, including an online database that could provide reliable information on registered organisations, their areas of operation and thematic focus.



The initiative is expected to strengthen transparency and accountability within Nigeria’s non-profit sector while helping legitimate organisations protect themselves against abuse by criminal and terrorist networks.



The EFCC and its partners maintained that effective terrorism-financing prevention requires a balanced approach that protects national security while ensuring that genuine humanitarian and development organisations can continue their legitimate activities without unnecessary disruption.
