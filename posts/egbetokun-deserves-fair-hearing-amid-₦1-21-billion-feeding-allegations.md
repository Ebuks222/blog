---
title: EGBETOKUN DESERVES FAIR HEARING AMID ₦1.21 BILLION FEEDING ALLEGATIONS
date: 2026-10-20T11:26:00.000+01:00
---
![](/assets/images/493809.jpg)

By Vivian Onwuasoanya



The allegations surrounding the reported ₦4.78 billion expenditure on feeding and dry rations by the Nigeria Police Force in 2024 have generated public interest, particularly following claims that ₦1.21 billion was paid to a supplier whose registration status has been questioned. However, amid the growing debate, it is important to separate allegations from established facts and ensure that the reputation of former Inspector-General of Police Kayode Egbetokun is not unfairly damaged by claims that have yet to be conclusively established.



While transparency and accountability remain essential in the management of public funds, fairness demands that individuals are not presumed guilty merely because questions have been raised about transactions carried out by a government institution during their tenure.



The report published by SecretsReporters alleges that Cloverfieds Limited received ₦1,212,492,196.74 through 16 payments from the police's reported feeding and dry-ration expenditure in 2024. It further claims that searches of the Corporate Affairs Commission's public register did not produce a registration record for the company.



These allegations deserve appropriate scrutiny. Nevertheless, the absence of a record in a particular search does not, by itself, conclusively establish that a company is unregistered, that the payments were unlawful or that any individual committed an offence.



More importantly, the reported transactions alone do not establish that Egbetokun personally authorised every payment, participated in any alleged irregularity or derived any personal benefit from the expenditure.



As the head of the Nigeria Police Force during the period in question, Egbetokun's tenure may understandably attract scrutiny over the institution's financial activities. However, a proper assessment must establish the relevant approval processes, the officials responsible for the transactions, the applicable procurement procedures and whether the goods for which payments were made were actually supplied.



It would be premature and unfair to turn questions about a supplier's registration status into a definitive judgment about the character or integrity of the former police chief without establishing the facts.



The principles of justice and responsible journalism require that Egbetokun be afforded a fair opportunity to respond to the allegations. The police authorities should also provide appropriate explanations and documentation to clarify the circumstances surrounding the payments.



Where irregularities are established, the law should take its course against those found responsible. Equally, where allegations cannot be substantiated, the public deserves an accurate account that prevents misinformation and unnecessary damage to individual reputations.



The issue should therefore be approached as a matter of public accountability requiring verification, rather than an opportunity to pass judgment before the relevant facts are known.



Egbetokun's public service and personal integrity should not be declared compromised solely on the strength of unverified allegations. At the same time, defending his reputation should not mean discouraging legitimate questions about public expenditure.



The proper position is simple: investigate the transactions, verify the documents, hear all relevant parties and allow the evidence to determine the outcome.



Nigeria needs a culture in which public officials are held accountable when wrongdoing is established, but are also protected from premature condemnation when allegations remain unresolved.



Until the facts are independently verified and responsibility established, the public should exercise caution in drawing conclusions about the former IGP.



Accountability and fairness are not opposing principles. Both are necessary for public confidence in the Nigeria Police Force and the institutions responsible for safeguarding public funds.
