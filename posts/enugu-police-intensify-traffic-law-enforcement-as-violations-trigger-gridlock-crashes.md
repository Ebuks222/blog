---
title: Enugu Police Intensify Traffic Law Enforcement as Violations Trigger
  Gridlock, Crashes
date: 2026-08-12T20:58:00.000+01:00
---
The Enugu State Police Command has commenced a stricter enforcement of traffic laws across the state following growing concerns over widespread violations by motorists and the attendant disruption to free movement on major roads.

The directive was issued by the Commissioner of Police, CP Mamman Bitrus Giwa, psc, mspsp, smicm, in response to the increasing disregard for traffic regulations, which the Command said has contributed to avoidable gridlock, obstruction of traffic flow, road crashes, loss of lives and destruction of property.

The renewed enforcement is also in compliance with the directive of the Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, psc (+), NPM, for nationwide enforcement of traffic laws as part of efforts to restore order on Nigerian roads and enhance the safety of motorists, commuters and other road users.

Under the directive, the Deputy Commissioner of Police in charge of Operations, the Command's Legal Officer and the State Traffic Officer have been tasked with ensuring professional and effective implementation of the enforcement measures.

The officers are expected to identify and deal with traffic offenders in accordance with extant laws and regulations, including the arrest and prosecution of persons who deliberately violate traffic rules.

For the Enugu Command, the renewed enforcement is not merely about penalising offenders but also about restoring discipline and responsibility on the state's roads. Traffic violations, particularly those that obstruct major roads or undermine the free movement of vehicles, can have consequences that extend beyond temporary inconvenience, contributing to congestion, crashes and preventable loss of lives.

CP Giwa has consequently urged motorists to take personal responsibility for road safety by familiarising themselves with existing traffic laws and complying strictly with them.

Importantly, the directive applies to everyone, including police officers and other security personnel. The Commissioner stressed that security personnel are not exempt from traffic regulations and must equally demonstrate responsible conduct on the roads.

At the same time, CP Giwa cautioned police officers involved in the enforcement exercise against infringing on the rights and dignity of citizens. The Command is expected to ensure that enforcement is carried out professionally, lawfully and with due regard for the rights of members of the public.

The Police Command believes that sustained compliance with traffic regulations will help reduce congestion, prevent road crashes and improve the overall movement of people and vehicles across Enugu State.

As enforcement intensifies, motorists have been advised to obey traffic signs, regulations and lawful directives from traffic personnel, with the warning that offenders risk arrest and prosecution in accordance with the law.

The development underscores the Command's broader responsibility to maintain public order while ensuring that the state's roads remain safe, accessible and conducive for daily economic and social activities.
