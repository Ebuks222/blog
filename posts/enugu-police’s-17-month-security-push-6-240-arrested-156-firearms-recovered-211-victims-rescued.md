---
title: "Enugu Police’s 17-Month Security Push: 6,240 Arrested, 156 Firearms
  Recovered, 211 Victims Rescued"
date: 2026-09-05T14:33:00.000+01:00
---
Security Connect Editorial



The Enugu State Police Command has, in the last 17 months, demonstrated what sustained operational pressure, intelligence-led policing and decisive leadership can achieve in the fight against crime.



With 6,240 suspects arrested, 156 firearms recovered, 1,486 rounds of ammunition seized and 211 kidnapped and abducted victims rescued, the Command has presented a security scorecard that deserves attention.



These are not merely figures on a police report. They represent thousands of encounters between law enforcement and criminal elements—and, most importantly, hundreds of families whose loved ones were rescued from the grip of kidnappers and abductors.



Since Mamman Bitrus Giwa assumed office as Commissioner of Police on March 11, 2025, the Enugu Command has intensified its operational posture against violent crime and criminality across the state.



The breakdown is revealing.



The Command arrested 158 kidnapping suspects, 186 armed robbery suspects, 188 murder suspects, 57 suspects linked to rape and defilement, 273 cultism suspects and 63 suspects for unlawful possession of firearms.



Another 5,315 suspects were arrested for various other offences, including stealing, breaking and vandalism.



The recovery of 156 firearms, including 43 AK-47 rifles and a G-3 rifle, together with 1,486 rounds of ammunition, is particularly significant.



Every illegal firearm removed from circulation potentially represents lives saved.



Every kidnap victim rescued represents a criminal operation disrupted.



Every criminal network dismantled represents another opportunity for citizens to live, work and move without fear.



This is why the 211 rescued victims stand out as one of the most important achievements in the Command’s scorecard.



Kidnapping has remained one of the most devastating security threats confronting communities across Nigeria. It destroys businesses, separates families, creates fear and weakens confidence in the ability of government to protect its citizens.



The rescue of 211 victims therefore goes beyond statistics. It is evidence that intelligence gathering, tactical operations, rapid response and inter-agency cooperation can produce tangible results.



The recovery of 209 vehicular exhibits, including motor vehicles, tricycles and motorcycles, further reflects the breadth of the Command’s operations.



But Security Connect believes the most important question is not simply how many suspects have been arrested.



The bigger question is: How effectively can these operations be converted into lasting security?



That is where the next phase of the Enugu security strategy becomes critical.



Criminals are constantly changing their methods. Kidnap syndicates reorganise. Cult groups recruit new members. Armed robbers change locations and tactics. Criminal networks exploit gaps in intelligence, borders and policing capacity.



The police must therefore remain several steps ahead.



The deployment of the Violent Crime Response Unit, intensified border patrols under the Inspector-General of Police’s Handshake Patrol initiative, and the strengthening of the Distress Response Squad and Command-and-Control Centre are steps in the right direction.



But these initiatives must be continuously strengthened with modern technology, adequate manpower, operational mobility and reliable intelligence.



This is why the appeal for additional manpower, logistics and improved welfare for police personnel should not be treated as a routine administrative request.



It is a security imperative.



An officer who is expected to confront an armed criminal network must have the tools, intelligence, mobility and institutional support required to do so.



At the same time, the emphasis by Miller Gajere Dantawaye on professionalism, discipline, accountability and respect for citizens' fundamental rights is equally important.



A strong police force must be both firm and professional.



The fight against criminals cannot become a licence for abuse. The police must remain within the law even when dealing with those who violate it.



Public trust is an operational asset.



When citizens trust the police, they provide information. They report suspicious movements. They cooperate with investigations. They become partners in the fight against crime.



When that relationship breaks down, criminals benefit.



For this reason, the Enugu Command must continue to build a policing model that combines aggressive action against criminality with respect for the people it protects.



The 17-month record under Giwa provides a solid foundation.



But the work is far from finished.



The real victory will not be measured only by arrests, recoveries or rescue operations. It will be measured by whether criminal networks are permanently weakened, whether communities become safer and whether residents can confidently go about their daily lives without the fear of kidnapping, robbery, cult violence or other forms of criminality.



The message from the Enugu Police Command is clear: the fight is ongoing.



The responsibility now is to sustain the pressure.



More intelligence.

More technology.

More manpower.

Better welfare.

Stronger community partnerships.

Greater professionalism.

And zero tolerance for criminality.



For Security Connect, the Enugu experience reinforces one fundamental truth:



Security gains must never be taken for granted. They must be consolidated, strengthened and transformed into lasting peace.



Enugu has recorded significant operational gains.



Now, those gains must become a safer Enugu.
