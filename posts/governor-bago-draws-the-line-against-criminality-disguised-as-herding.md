---
title: Governor Bago Draws the Line Against Criminality Disguised as Herding
date: 2026-08-04T20:35:00.000+01:00
---
For years, communities across Niger State and many parts of northern Nigeria have lived under the shadow of insecurity. What were once thriving farming communities have, in many cases, become scenes of fear, displacement, and economic hardship. Bandit attacks, violent clashes, kidnappings, and the destruction of farmlands have claimed countless lives, displaced thousands of families, and threatened food security.



Farmers have repeatedly watched months of hard work destroyed overnight as crops were trampled by cattle or abandoned because of insecurity. Many have been unable to return to their farms, while entire communities have suffered devastating attacks from armed criminal groups operating under the guise of herders or exploiting long-standing farmer-herder tensions.



Against this backdrop, the recent remarks by Niger State Governor Umar Bago represent a firm declaration that the era of impunity must come to an end. Speaking during a meeting with Emirs and traditional rulers, the governor warned that anyone found destroying farmlands or farm produce would face the full weight of the law, including possible imprisonment. He also vowed that any mosque used to spread hatred or division would be shut down, while religious leaders preaching messages capable of inciting violence would be closely monitored.



Governor Bago emphasized that criminality should never be shielded by ethnicity or religion. Identifying himself as a Fulani man, he made it clear that no one should use ethnic identity as an excuse to commit crimes or tarnish the reputation of an entire community. His message was unequivocal: anyone who cannot live peacefully, obey the law, and respect the rights of others has no place in Niger State.



The governor also urged herders to graze responsibly and advised farmers to observe recommended setbacks from major roads and electricity corridors to help reduce avoidable conflicts. More importantly, he reaffirmed his administration's commitment to protecting both farmers and law-abiding herders while ensuring that those who engage in violence, destroy livelihoods, or threaten public peace are held accountable.



As Nigeria continues to grapple with insecurity, many citizens believe that restoring law and order requires firm, impartial leadership. Regardless of ethnic background or occupation, every individual must be subject to the law. Protecting lives, preserving livelihoods, and fostering peaceful coexistence remain essential to rebuilding confidence in affected communities and securing the nation's future.
