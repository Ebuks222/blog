---
title: How ICT Is Transforming Crime Investigation in the Nigeria Police Force
  As IGP Disu Charges Investigators to Embrace Data-Driven Policing as NPF
  Strengthens Crime Database Management
date: 2026-08-07T09:04:00.000+01:00
---
The Nigeria Police Force is strengthening its transition towards technology-driven and intelligence-led policing, with the Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, psc(+), NPM, charging investigators across the country to prioritise the accurate capture, management and utilisation of suspects' information.

The IGP gave the charge on June 10, 2026, while declaring open a One-Day Crime and Incident Database Impact Sensitisation Workshop organised by the Department of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) at the Force Headquarters, Abuja.

The workshop was organised to sensitise investigators and heads of investigative units on the importance of the Suspects' Information Capturing Process under the Nigeria Police Force Crime and Incident Database (NPF-ICD) Centre Project, an initiative designed to strengthen crime analysis, intelligence gathering, investigations, inter-agency collaboration and proactive crime prevention.

The initiative comes at a time when the nature of crime is becoming increasingly sophisticated and criminal networks are operating across geographical boundaries. In such an environment, policing can no longer depend solely on conventional investigative methods or information stored in isolated files. The ability to capture, store, retrieve and analyse information has become an essential part of modern law enforcement.

Speaking at the workshop, IGP Disu emphasised that timely, accurate and actionable data is now central to effective policing. He noted that the Crime and Incident Database Centre represents an important national policing asset capable of helping investigators identify criminal trends, track repeat offenders, link seemingly unrelated crimes and provide commanders with reliable information for operational and strategic decision-making.

The significance of the initiative also highlights the increasingly important role of the Department of Information and Communication Technology within the Nigeria Police Force. Under the leadership of the Deputy Inspector-General of Police in charge of ICT, DIG Fayoade Adegoke Mustapha, mni, the department remains an important pillar in the Force's ongoing digital transformation.

ICT has become far more than a technical support unit within modern policing. It provides the technological foundation for communication, information management, databases, digital systems and other technology-enabled policing capabilities. As criminal activities increasingly exploit technology, the Police must equally strengthen their ability to use technology to prevent, investigate and respond to crime.

The NPF-ICD Centre Project therefore represents an important component of this transformation. By developing a more coordinated system for capturing suspects' and incident information, the Force can strengthen its ability to connect information generated from different investigations and locations.

For investigators, accurate data can provide a clearer picture of a suspect's history and possible links to other offences. It can also help reveal patterns that may not be immediately visible when individual cases are examined separately.

For commanders, reliable data can support better deployment of personnel and resources. For intelligence officers, it can provide a stronger foundation for analysing criminal trends. And for the wider policing system, it can improve collaboration between Commands and Formations.

However, the effectiveness of any database depends heavily on the quality of the information entered into it. An inaccurate name, incomplete personal information or improperly recorded incident can compromise the usefulness of the system. This explains the IGP's emphasis on compliance with established procedures for capturing suspects' information.

IGP Disu urged participants to take full advantage of the workshop and to share the knowledge gained with officers in their respective Commands and Formations. The objective, he noted, is to establish a common understanding and ensure that proper data-capture procedures become an integral part of police investigations.

The importance of ICT in this process cannot be overstated. Technology does not replace the police investigator; rather, it equips the investigator with better tools to perform the job more efficiently. A database may contain information, but it is the professionalism, experience and analytical ability of police personnel that ultimately turn that information into actionable intelligence.

The increasing use of technology in policing also offers opportunities for the Force to move from a largely reactive approach to a more proactive model of crime prevention. By analysing patterns and identifying emerging threats, police authorities can potentially anticipate criminal activities and deploy resources more strategically.

This is particularly important in tackling crimes such as kidnapping, terrorism, organised crime, cybercrime, armed robbery and other offences that frequently involve networks operating across different locations.

The workshop therefore goes beyond the technical process of entering information into a database. It forms part of a broader effort to build a modern police institution where decisions are increasingly supported by evidence, intelligence and reliable information.

As the Nigeria Police Force continues to invest in technology and innovation, the Department of ICT will remain central to the success of that transformation. Its ability to provide the systems, infrastructure, technical expertise and support required by investigators will be critical to ensuring that technology becomes a practical tool for improving policing outcomes.

The message from IGP Disu is clear: the future of effective policing depends not only on the number of officers deployed to the streets but also on the quality of information available to those officers.

By strengthening its crime database management and encouraging investigators to embrace data-driven policing, the Nigeria Police Force is taking another step towards building a more intelligent, responsive and technology-enabled policing system.

Ultimately, the goal is simple—to ensure that information gathered by the Police does not remain locked away in files, but becomes intelligence capable of helping officers prevent crime, solve cases, identify offenders, protect communities and make Nigeria safer.
