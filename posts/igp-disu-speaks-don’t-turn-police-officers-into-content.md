---
title: "IGP Disu Speaks: Don’t Turn Police Officers Into Content"
date: 2026-09-10T22:45:00.000+01:00
---
The Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, has spoken on an issue that has increasingly generated concern within policing circles and the wider public: the recording of police officers while carrying out their official duties.



For years, officers have been filmed during arrests, stop-and-search operations, traffic enforcement, investigations and other sensitive assignments, with some of the footage later edited, mocked or circulated on social media primarily for entertainment and engagement.



While citizens have a legitimate interest in police accountability, the growing practice of turning officers and sensitive police operations into social-media content raises important questions about operational security, privacy, dignity and public safety.



Police officers are not content.



Security Connect welcomes the position expressed by the IGP and commends him for drawing attention to the need for responsible conduct around police operations.



However, this conversation should not end with a speech.



The Nigeria Police Force needs a clear, lawful and publicly understandable framework explaining when members of the public may record police officers, what constitutes interference with police duties, and what restrictions may apply around sensitive operations.



Such guidelines would protect both sides.



Citizens should not be prevented from documenting legitimate incidents or reporting alleged misconduct. At the same time, police officers should not be subjected to harassment, deliberate provocation, humiliation or interference simply because someone wants to create viral content.



There is also a critical distinction between recording for accountability and recording for entertainment.



A citizen documenting an alleged act of police misconduct is fundamentally different from someone deliberately provoking an officer, obstructing an arrest, revealing the identity or location of a sensitive operation, filming victims or suspects in vulnerable circumstances, or turning an ongoing security operation into a social-media spectacle.



Operational security must also be taken seriously.



A police officer responding to an emergency, conducting an investigation, operating in a high-risk environment or participating in a covert or sensitive assignment cannot be expected to perform under the same conditions as a public figure appearing before cameras.



A viral video may last only a few minutes online, but the consequences of exposing police tactics, locations, identities, evidence or vulnerable persons can extend far beyond social-media engagement.



This does not mean the police should operate without scrutiny.



On the contrary, accountability remains essential to public confidence. Police officers are public servants and must operate within the law. Where misconduct occurs, there must be appropriate mechanisms for citizens to report it and for the authorities to investigate.



But accountability must not become harassment, while transparency must not become operational recklessness.



There is therefore a need for the Nigeria Police Force, relevant government institutions and civil-society stakeholders to engage on a practical framework that balances citizens' rights with police safety, operational security and public interest.



Clear rules would also protect police officers from uncertainty.



An officer should know what is permitted, what constitutes unlawful interference and what steps can legally be taken when filming genuinely threatens an operation. Equally, members of the public should know their rights and responsibilities when recording police activity.



The objective should not be to silence citizens.



The objective should be to ensure that neither the police nor the public becomes a victim of irresponsible recording.



A police officer on a highway, at a crime scene, in a forest, at a checkpoint or responding to an emergency is carrying out a serious public duty. That duty should not be reduced to entertainment for TikTok, Facebook, YouTube or any other platform.



POLICE OFFICERS ARE NOT CONTENT.



Security Connect therefore welcomes the IGP's intervention and urges the Force to take the next step by developing clear, lawful and transparent guidelines on recording police officers and police operations.



The line should be drawn where recording crosses into obstruction, harassment, exposure of sensitive security information, violation of privacy or interference with lawful police duties.



At the same time, legitimate documentation of police conduct should remain an important part of responsible civic accountability.



The balance is possible.



Respect the Police. Protect the Public. Uphold the Law.



Security Connect calls for responsible recording, lawful accountability and clear rules that protect both citizens and police officers.
