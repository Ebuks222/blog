---
title: Imo CP Orders Tighter Security, Warns Vigilantes Against Unlawful
  Detention, Extrajudicial Killings By Vivian Onwuasoanya
date: 2026-10-21T22:47:00.000+01:00
---
The Commissioner of Police in Imo State, CP Audu Garba Bosso, has directed Area Commanders and Divisional Police Officers (DPOs) to strengthen intelligence sharing, improve patrol coordination and tighten supervision of vigilantes as the state prepares for the festive season and forthcoming elections.



Bosso gave the directives on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, during a strategic security meeting with Area Commanders, DPOs and members of the Imo State Security Organization (IMSO), alongside other security stakeholders.



The meeting focused on strengthening collaboration between the police and community-based security groups, addressing operational challenges and enhancing proactive measures to protect lives and property across the state.



The police commissioner also warned security personnel against extrajudicial killings, extortion, unlawful detention, harassment and other forms of misconduct, stressing the need for professionalism and respect for human rights in the discharge of their duties.



He emphasized the importance of vigilantes in community security, noting their proximity to residents and their potential to provide timely intelligence that could help prevent crime.



Bosso consequently directed vigilantes to work closely with the police and ensure that anyone apprehended was promptly handed over to the appropriate authorities in line with established procedures.



Representing Governor Hope Uzodimma at the meeting, the Chief Principal Security Officer to the governor, ACP Gboyako Shaba Adamu, conveyed the governor's directives on community security, particularly the need for accountability among vigilante groups.



Adamu stressed that vigilantes must remain answerable to their respective DPOs and refrain from conducting unauthorised operations. He also warned that they must not operate detention facilities, emphasising that strict compliance with the law was essential to effective community policing.



Similarly, the Commissioner for Homeland Security and Vigilante Affairs was represented by Barrister Ihezue Samuel Chukwudi, who underscored the importance of sustained cooperation between formal security agencies and community-based security structures in tackling security challenges.



The Commander of IMSO was represented by the State Administrator, Mr Nwachukwu Valentine.



Other participants included members of the police command's management team, Area Commanders, DPOs, vigilante coordinators from all 27 local government areas of Imo State and other relevant security stakeholders.



An interactive session provided an opportunity for participants to discuss operational difficulties and identify practical ways to improve intelligence sharing, coordination and accountability in the activities of vigilante groups.



The meeting comes as security agencies intensify preparations for the festive season and forthcoming elections, periods that often require heightened vigilance and effective collaboration among security stakeholders.



The Imo State Police Command reaffirmed its commitment to proactive policing and the protection of lives and property, urging residents to remain vigilant and promptly report suspicious activities to the appropriate authorities.
