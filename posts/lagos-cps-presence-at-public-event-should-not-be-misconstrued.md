---
title: Lagos CP's Presence at Public Event Should Not Be Misconstrued
date: 2026-07-29T14:27:00.000+01:00
---
The recent controversy surrounding the presence of the Commissioner of Police, Lagos State Command, CP Tijani Fatai, at the inauguration of the City Boy Movement's Lagos chapter has generated intense public debate. While differing opinions are expected in a democratic society, it is important that the facts and statutory responsibilities of the Nigeria Police Force are considered before drawing conclusions.

As the primary agency responsible for maintaining law and order, the Nigeria Police Force has a constitutional duty to provide security at public gatherings where there is a potential need to protect lives and property. Senior police officers routinely attend major events to assess security arrangements, coordinate deployments, or respond to invitations in their official capacity. Such appearances do not, by themselves, amount to political endorsement or affiliation.

The viral video circulating online captures only a brief moment and does not, on its own, establish that the Commissioner participated in political activities or expressed support for any political group. Without evidence of partisan conduct, it would be premature and unfair to conclude that his attendance constituted a breach of professional neutrality.

It is also worth noting that police officers across the country provide security for events organised by political parties, civil society organisations, religious bodies, traditional institutions and other interest groups. Their presence is intended to ensure public safety, prevent violence and maintain order, irrespective of the organisers' political or social affiliations.

Until the Nigeria Police Force issues an official statement or credible evidence emerges indicating that the Commissioner acted outside the scope of his official duties, the presumption should be that he was carrying out his constitutional responsibility of safeguarding public order.

In a democracy governed by the rule of law, accountability is essential, but so too is fairness. Public officials should be judged on verifiable facts rather than assumptions based solely on photographs or short video clips. Any allegation of misconduct should be investigated through established institutional processes, ensuring that conclusions are based on evidence and due process rather than public speculation.
