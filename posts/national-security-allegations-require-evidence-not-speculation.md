---
title: National Security Allegations Require Evidence, Not Speculation
date: 2026-08-26T20:53:00.000+01:00
---
Serious allegations involving national security and the integrity of security agencies should never be reduced to mere public commentary or treated as entertainment. Claims that police officers are providing information to kidnappers are grave allegations that, if substantiated, could have far-reaching implications for public safety and the fight against criminality.



The Nigeria Police Force and other security agencies continue to operate in an increasingly challenging environment, with officers routinely confronting kidnappers, armed criminals and other violent threats. Many personnel have lost their lives in the line of duty while protecting communities and maintaining law and order.



Against this backdrop, allegations of an alleged insider network within the police must be handled with responsibility. If VDM possesses credible evidence that serving police officers are deliberately supplying intelligence to kidnappers, such evidence should be presented to the appropriate authorities for thorough investigation. Specific information, documents, communications or other verifiable material would provide a proper basis for establishing the truth.



Criticism of security agencies is legitimate in a democratic society, particularly where there are concerns about operational failures or misconduct. However, there is an important distinction between constructive criticism and making unsubstantiated allegations that could undermine public confidence in institutions entrusted with protecting lives and property.



Security agencies should also remain open to credible information from members of the public. Where genuine wrongdoing is alleged, the appropriate authorities should investigate without fear or favour and, where evidence establishes culpability, ensure that those responsible face the law.



At the same time, the sacrifices of the vast majority of police officers and other security personnel should not be dismissed because of allegations against individuals. An allegation against some personnel, even when credible, should not automatically become an indictment of an entire institution.



The fight against kidnapping and other violent crimes requires cooperation between citizens, the police, the military, intelligence agencies and other relevant institutions. That cooperation depends heavily on accurate information, responsible public communication and trust.



When the subject is national security, words carry consequences. Allegations should therefore be supported by facts and subjected to proper investigation rather than amplified without verification.



The message is straightforward: if there is evidence of criminal collaboration within the security architecture, it should be produced and investigated. If there is wrongdoing, those responsible should be held accountable. But without evidence, serious accusations risk creating fear, weakening public confidence and distracting attention from the genuine security challenges facing the country.



The men and women who risk their lives every day deserve accountability where they fail, but they also deserve fairness and recognition for the difficult work they perform in defence of the public.
