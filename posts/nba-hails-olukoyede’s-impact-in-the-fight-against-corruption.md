---
title: NBA hails Olukoyede’s impact in the fight against corruption
date: 2026-09-17T00:14:00.000+01:00
category: FIGHT AGAINST CORRUPTION
---


![](/assets/images/387395.jpg)

The fight against corruption is one that requires more than investigations and prosecutions. It demands strong institutions, professional collaboration, preventive measures and a sustained commitment to the rule of law.



Against this backdrop, the commendation recently extended to the Executive Chairman of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), Mr. Ola Olukoyede, by the President of the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA), Mrs. Oyinkansola Badejo-Okusanya, SAN, is significant.



During a courtesy visit to the EFCC headquarters in Abuja on Wednesday, September 16, 2026, the NBA President acknowledged the Commission’s efforts in investigating and prosecuting economic and financial crimes, recovering proceeds of crime and strengthening Nigeria’s response to financial crimes.



Her remarks also highlight an important reality: the fight against corruption cannot be the responsibility of one institution alone. It requires the cooperation of law-enforcement agencies, the legal profession, financial institutions, regulators, the private sector and the wider Nigerian public.



Under Olukoyede’s leadership, the EFCC has increasingly emphasised not only the prosecution of offenders but also the prevention of financial crimes. This preventive approach is particularly important in a country where criminal networks continually devise new ways to exploit gaps within financial and institutional systems.



Olukoyede’s decision to strengthen preventive mechanisms within the EFCC, including the establishment of the Fraud Risk Assessment and Control (FRAC) Department, reflects the understanding that preventing criminal proceeds from being generated or moved in the first place can be as important as recovering them after a crime has occurred.



The EFCC Chairman has also repeatedly stressed that the Commission must operate within the confines of the law and remain accountable to the public. That commitment to due process is central to the credibility of any anti-corruption institution. The fight against financial crime must be firm, but it must equally be anchored on legality, evidence and respect for the rule of law.



Nigeria’s removal from the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) grey list in October 2025 provides another important point of reference. FATF formally removed Nigeria from increased monitoring after the country completed its action plan addressing identified deficiencies in its anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing framework.



That achievement was not the work of the EFCC alone. It involved collaboration among several Nigerian institutions and stakeholders. However, the EFCC’s role in strengthening investigations, prosecutions, financial intelligence and asset recovery formed an important part of the broader institutional effort.



The NBA’s recognition of this progress therefore comes at a time when stronger cooperation between the legal profession and anti-graft agencies remains necessary.



The legal profession occupies a particularly important position in the fight against economic crime. Lawyers are central to the justice system, corporate transactions, property dealings and other areas that can either strengthen financial transparency or, if abused, provide opportunities for illicit financial activity.



This makes the proposed closer collaboration between the NBA and EFCC particularly relevant.



Knowledge sharing, professional training, stronger compliance practices and improved understanding of anti-money-laundering obligations can help build a more effective institutional response to economic and financial crimes.



The EFCC, for its part, must continue to demonstrate that its war against corruption is not merely about headlines, arrests or recoveries. Sustainable anti-corruption work is ultimately about building systems that make financial crime more difficult to commit and easier to detect, while ensuring that investigations and prosecutions are conducted fairly and transparently.



Olukoyede deserves acknowledgement for keeping the fight against economic and financial crimes firmly on the national agenda and for placing greater emphasis on prevention alongside enforcement.



But the task ahead remains collective.



As the NBA President rightly noted, the objective should be the creation of a lawful and orderly society capable of supporting economic growth, protecting legitimate businesses and improving the quality of life of Nigerians.



The EFCC cannot win that battle alone. Neither can the NBA, the police, the judiciary, financial regulators or any other institution acting in isolation.



What Nigeria needs is a sustained institutional partnership—one in which every stakeholder plays its part in strengthening the rule of law, protecting the economy and making corruption and financial crime increasingly difficult to sustain.



The message from the EFCC-NBA engagement is therefore clear: the fight against corruption must continue, but it must also evolve.



And as that fight evolves, prevention, professionalism, accountability, inter-agency cooperation and respect for the law must remain at its centre.
