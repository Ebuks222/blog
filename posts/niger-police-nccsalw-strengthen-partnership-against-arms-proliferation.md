---
title: Niger Police, NCCSALW Strengthen Partnership Against Arms Proliferation
date: 2026-08-18T22:23:00.000+01:00
---
The Commissioner of Police, Niger State Command, CP Adamu Abdullahi Elleman, mnips, has reaffirmed the Command’s commitment to strengthening collaboration with the National Centre for the Control of Small Arms and Light Weapons (NCCSALW) to tackle the proliferation of illicit arms and ammunition in the state.

CP Elleman made the commitment on Tuesday, August 18, 2026, when he received the Director-General of the NCCSALW, DIG Johnson Babatunde Kokumo (Rtd), mni, Ph.D, NPM, who paid a courtesy visit to the Command Headquarters in Dutsen-Kura, Minna.

The visit, which formed part of the Director-General’s tour of the North Central Zone, also provided an opportunity to further deepen the existing relationship between the Niger State Police Command and the Centre’s North Central Zonal Office.

DIG Kokumo commended the cooperation between both institutions, stressing that effective information sharing and inter-agency collaboration remain critical to confronting the proliferation of small arms and light weapons that continue to fuel banditry and other violent crimes across the country.

He also commended the Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, psc(+), NPM, for his support to the Centre, revealing that the Nigeria Police Force had so far remitted more than 11,000 recovered arms to the NCCSALW for destruction, thereby preventing them from returning to criminal networks.

Responding, CP Elleman described the visit as a significant step towards strengthening the partnership between the Police and NCCSALW. He described DIG Kokumo as a distinguished retired police officer, mentor and seasoned professional whose career was defined by integrity, transparency and excellence.

The Commissioner assured the Centre that the Command would continue to support its mandate and ensure that all recovered arms and ammunition, after the completion of investigations and other necessary procedures, are duly transferred to the Centre through the appropriate channels.

With Niger State facing persistent security challenges linked to banditry and the movement of illicit arms, the renewed partnership is expected to enhance intelligence sharing, arms recovery and coordinated security operations, while helping to prevent recovered weapons from finding their way back into the hands of criminal elements.

The engagement underscores the growing importance of a coordinated approach among security agencies in addressing the arms supply chain that sustains violent crime and threatens peace and stability in the North Central region.
