---
title: Nigeria Customs, Afreximbank Deepen Talks on Regional Border Modernisation
date: 2026-10-19T16:29:00.000+01:00
---
By Vivian Onwuasoanya



The Nigeria Customs Service (NCS) has intensified discussions with the African Export-Import Bank (Afreximbank) and the Customs administrations of Benin and Cameroon on plans to modernise key regional border corridors to facilitate trade, improve cross-border efficiency and advance the objectives of the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA).



The discussions took place on October 12, 2026, at Afreximbank’s headquarters in Cairo, Egypt, during a high-level working engagement led by the Comptroller-General of Customs, Bashir Adewale Adeniyi.



The meeting followed a joint benchmarking mission to the Beitbridge Border Post in Zimbabwe and subsequent technical assessments of the Mfum–Ekok and Sèmè–Kraké border corridors, which identified critical infrastructure and operational gaps requiring urgent attention.



Representatives of the three Customs administrations, Afreximbank and technical partners participated in the engagement to review the assessment findings, identify priority interventions and explore practical approaches to developing an integrated regional border-modernisation programme.



The proposed initiative seeks to improve border operations through digital infrastructure, harmonised Customs procedures, secure data exchange and institutional capacity development, alongside investments in cargo scanners, weighbridges, access roads, electricity, connectivity, traffic management and modern border-processing facilities.



Participants also discussed Afreximbank’s potential involvement in the next Customs Partnership for African Cooperation in Trade (C-PACT) and progress towards establishing regional and continental transit-guarantee arrangements.



Digital Integration at the Heart of Border Modernisation



A major focus of the discussions was the Customs Declaration Exchange System (CDES), a digital platform designed to facilitate secure information exchange among Nigeria’s B’Odogwu platform, Benin’s Customs Webb and Cameroon’s CAMCIS.



The system is expected to enable export information generated in one country to support the advance preparation of import-manifest information in the receiving country, reducing duplication, improving information sharing and making cross-border transactions more predictable.



Under the proposed arrangement, the receiving Customs administration will retain responsibility for validating information, enforcing national requirements, conducting risk assessments and authorising cargo clearance.



Although development and technical testing of the system have been undertaken, further country-specific configuration, system integration, security assurance and pilot acceptance are required before deployment.



The initiative is expected to strengthen digital cooperation among the three countries and support more coordinated border management.



Assessments Identify Critical Infrastructure Gaps



Findings from the July 2026 benchmarking mission to Beitbridge and subsequent assessments of the two priority corridors highlighted the importance of combining modern infrastructure with effective operational coordination.



The Beitbridge experience demonstrated how traffic management, pre-arrival processing, risk-based controls, cargo scanning, transit monitoring, electronic payments and information technology command centres can contribute to more efficient border operations.



However, the assessments identified significant challenges at the Mfum–Ekok and Sèmè–Kraké corridors.



At Mfum–Ekok, the identified gaps included unreliable electricity, inadequate connectivity and information technology equipment, cybersecurity concerns, insufficient technical support, delays in scanner commissioning and the need for better-coordinated single-stop border processing.

Similarly, the Sèmè–Kraké corridor faces challenges involving incomplete joint operating procedures, limited live data exchange, non-functional cargo-scanning and weighing facilities, unreliable power supply, traffic congestion and inconsistent operating hours.



Unresolved responsibilities for maintaining shared infrastructure were also identified as an obstacle to effective border operations.



The findings underscored the need to address immediate operational deficiencies while developing a properly assessed and costed long-term modernisation programme.



Afreximbank Proposes Two-Stage Project Preparation



To move the initiative forward, Afreximbank outlined a proposed two-stage approach beginning with independent engineering assessments and investment-cost studies at the two border posts.



The second stage would involve developing separate bankable project packages for the Nigeria–Benin and Nigeria–Cameroon corridors.



The proposed project-preparation process would cover transaction advisory services, legal due diligence, financial modelling and the identification of appropriate implementation and financing arrangements.



Afreximbank also outlined the potential use of its Project Preparation Facility to support the process.



Meanwhile, technical partners, TMP and Bergmans Security Consultants and Supplies Limited, expressed willingness in principle to consider self-financing the preparatory work.



However, the meeting did not establish a final project cost, lending commitment, designated borrower, financing arrangement or implementation contract.



Participants instead agreed on the importance of conducting direct site inspections, appointing designated technical focal points and developing a clear implementation roadmap covering one, three and six months, supported by a dated action matrix.



These measures are expected to provide a framework for coordinating subsequent activities and clarifying the responsibilities of the participating institutions.



NCS Reaffirms Commitment to Regional Trade Facilitation



The Nigeria Customs Service reaffirmed its commitment to strengthening regional cooperation, modernising border operations and facilitating legitimate trade in line with the objectives of AfCFTA.



Under the leadership of Adeniyi and his management team, the Service said it would continue engaging with its counterparts in Benin and Cameroon, Afreximbank and relevant technical partners to advance the required assessments and clarify project-preparation arrangements.



The broader objective is to develop practical and sustainable solutions that will improve cross-border procedures, enhance cargo visibility, strengthen coordinated border management and support the development of an integrated African trading system.



If successfully implemented, the proposed programme could help address longstanding operational challenges at the two border corridors while improving the movement of goods and supporting trade among participating African countries.



The next phase will depend on the completion of technical assessments, agreement on project-preparation arrangements and the development of bankable proposals capable of attracting the investment required for implementation.
