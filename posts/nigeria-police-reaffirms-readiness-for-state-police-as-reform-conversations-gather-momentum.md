---
title: Nigeria Police Reaffirms Readiness for State Police as Reform
  Conversations Gather Momentum
date: 2026-08-06T21:08:00.000+01:00
---
The Nigeria Police Force has reiterated its commitment to supporting the implementation of State Police, should the proposed policing model become a reality, while stressing that professionalism, accountability, and operational excellence must remain the foundation of the reform.

This position was reaffirmed by the Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, during the opening of a One-Day Sensitisation Workshop on State Police organised by the Nigeria Institute of Police Studies (NIPS) in Abuja.

The workshop brought together senior police officers and stakeholders to deepen understanding of the proposed State Police framework and prepare the Force for possible changes in Nigeria's policing structure. It also provided a platform for constructive engagement on one of the country's most significant security reform conversations.

Declaring the workshop open, the Inspector-General described the initiative as timely and strategic, noting that it would equip officers with the knowledge and capacity required to contribute meaningfully to discussions on the future of policing in Nigeria.

He emphasised that while the debate on State Police continues at the national level, the Nigeria Police Force remains committed to ensuring that any eventual implementation is guided by global best practices, professionalism, transparency, and accountability. According to him, these principles are essential to preserving public trust and strengthening national security.

The IGP further urged participants to actively engage in the deliberations, asking them to see themselves as ambassadors of the reform process by sharing the knowledge gained with officers in their respective commands and formations. Such collaboration, he noted, would help create a common understanding of the proposed reforms across the Force and ensure seamless adaptation to any future policing framework.

In his welcome address, the Director-General of the Nigeria Institute of Police Studies, Professor Olu Ogunshakin, commended the Inspector-General for his proactive leadership in establishing the Nigeria Police Force Steering Committee on State Police.

Professor Ogunshakin disclosed that several recommendations made by the committee have already found expression in the proposed State Police Bill, describing the committee's contributions as a significant milestone in Nigeria's ongoing policing reform efforts.

He noted that the workshop reflects the commitment of the Nigeria Police Force to remain actively involved in shaping reforms that will improve policing efficiency, strengthen institutional capacity, and enhance public confidence in law enforcement.

The sensitisation programme is part of the Force's broader strategy to prepare its personnel for emerging reforms and evolving security challenges. By investing in capacity building and policy awareness, the Nigeria Police Force aims to ensure that officers are well-positioned to effectively implement future reforms while maintaining the high standards of service expected by Nigerians.

As conversations on State Police continue to attract national attention, the workshop underscores the Force's readiness to contribute constructively to the reform process and its determination to ensure that any new policing architecture ultimately enhances security, improves service delivery, and strengthens the nation's criminal justice system.
