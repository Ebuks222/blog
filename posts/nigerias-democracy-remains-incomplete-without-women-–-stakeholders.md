---
title: Nigeria's Democracy Remains Incomplete Without Women – Stakeholders
date: 2026-07-30T15:19:00.000+01:00
---
Stakeholders have called for greater inclusion of women in Nigeria's political and leadership spaces, insisting that the nation's democracy cannot reach its full potential while women remain underrepresented in decision-making positions.

The call was made during the SALTi Conversations Series, organised by the Strategic Action for Leadership Trust Initiative (SALTi) in collaboration with the National Association of Women Journalists (NAWOJ), under the theme: "Why Nigeria Needs More Women at the Table" in Abuja.

Speaking at the event, the Regional Director for West Africa at the Ford Foundation, ChiChi Aniagolu-Okoye, lamented the low representation of women in parliament, noting that women occupy less than five per cent of parliamentary seats despite making up nearly half of Nigeria's population. She stressed that excluding women from leadership deprives the country of valuable talent, innovation, and diverse perspectives needed for national development.

Also speaking, Ogbechie, Deputy Editor of Blueprint Newspaper and NAWOJ National Vice President (Zone D), highlighted the structural barriers preventing women from participating effectively in politics. According to her, high nomination fees, limited access to campaign financing, weak implementation of gender inclusion policies, and entrenched party structures continue to shut women out of political leadership.

She noted that while women play active roles during election campaigns by mobilising voters and supporting candidates, they are often excluded from key decision-making processes and leadership positions.

Participants agreed that Nigeria's economic, social, and security challenges require inclusive leadership that reflects the country's diversity. They advocated stronger enforcement of gender-inclusive policies, financial support for female candidates, political party reforms, and sustained advocacy to create a level playing field for women.

The consensus at the forum was clear: Nigeria's democracy will remain incomplete until women are not only represented but empowered to influence policies and shape the nation's future.
