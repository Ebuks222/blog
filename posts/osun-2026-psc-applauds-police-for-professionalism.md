---
title: "Osun 2026: PSC Applauds Police for Professionalism"
date: 2026-08-16T17:12:00.000+01:00
---
The conduct of the Nigeria Police Force during the 2026 Osun State Governorship Election has received a positive assessment from the Police Service Commission (PSC), with the Commission describing the deployment and general conduct of officers across the state as professional, orderly and cordial.

For a state preparing for a closely watched governorship election, the visible presence of security personnel was critical to maintaining public confidence and ensuring that voters could participate in the democratic process without unnecessary disruption.

The PSC’s interim assessment followed monitoring carried out across the three senatorial districts—Osun Central, Osun East and Osun West—and the 30 Local Government Areas of the state.

The Commission’s monitoring teams assessed police deployment, professionalism, compliance with election duty guidelines, interaction with voters and stakeholders, inter-agency cooperation and the general conduct of officers at polling locations.

According to the interim report, police deployment was considered adequate and strategic, averaging four officers per polling unit across the state.

Osun Central recorded an average of three officers per polling unit, while Osun East and Osun West recorded an average of five officers each.

Beyond numbers, however, the Commission said what stood out was the manner in which many officers conducted themselves.

Police personnel observed across the three senatorial districts were generally described as courteous, approachable and professional. Officers were also reported to have maintained order and responded appropriately to questions and inquiries from monitoring teams.

For the PSC, this aspect of police conduct is particularly important because election security is not simply about the number of officers deployed. It is also about how those officers exercise their authority.

A professional officer can help calm tension, reassure voters and prevent minor disagreements from escalating into larger security incidents.

The election also witnessed cooperation among several security and safety agencies. Personnel of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, Department of State Services, Nigeria Immigration Service, Federal Road Safety Corps, Federal Fire Service, Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission, Nigeria Correctional Service, Nigerian Army, Nigerian Air Force, Nigerian Navy and Defence Intelligence Agency were observed working alongside the Police at various locations.

The PSC said the inter-agency collaboration was cordial and contributed significantly to the peaceful conduct of the election.

Leading the Commission’s monitoring team, DIG (Dr) Taiwo Lakanu (rtd) expressed satisfaction with the conduct of the Police and commended the Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, for the deployment strategy adopted for the election.

He also acknowledged the roles played by DIG Isyaku Mohammed, Ph.D, who served as the Election DIG, and CP Samuel Erale Etaifo, the Election CP, alongside other experienced officers deployed for the exercise.

The positive assessment comes at a time when public expectations of election security remain high. Nigerians want security agencies to protect voters, maintain order and remain professional without interfering with the democratic choices of citizens.

The PSC’s interim findings suggest that, in Osun, the Police largely met that responsibility.

For the people of Osun State, the peaceful atmosphere recorded during the election offers an important reminder that effective security planning, disciplined deployment and strong cooperation among agencies can make a significant difference.

It also demonstrates why professionalism must remain at the heart of election policing.

While the PSC described its report as an interim assessment, the Commission has reaffirmed its commitment to continued oversight of police conduct during electoral duties and ensuring that officers operate in line with established professional standards.

The Osun experience therefore provides an encouraging example of what can happen when security agencies combine strategic deployment with courtesy, coordination and respect for citizens.

At the end of every election, the results may determine the political direction of a state, but the manner in which voters are protected and treated also matters.

In Osun, the PSC says the Police gave Nigerians a reason to acknowledge the importance of professionalism in safeguarding democracy.
