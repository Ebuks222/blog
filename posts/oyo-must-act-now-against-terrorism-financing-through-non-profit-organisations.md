---
title: Oyo Must Act Now Against Terrorism Financing Through Non-Profit Organisations
date: 2026-09-10T22:49:00.000+01:00
---
The warning by the Executive Chairman of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission, EFCC, Mr. Ola Olukoyede, to the Oyo State Government on the need to enact a law regulating the registration, monitoring and supervision of Non-Profit Organisations, NPOs, deserves urgent attention.



It is a warning that goes beyond Oyo State. It speaks directly to Nigeria’s evolving security reality and the disturbing possibility that legitimate humanitarian structures could be exploited to finance terrorism.



Non-profit organisations play an important role in society. They provide humanitarian assistance, support vulnerable communities, contribute to healthcare delivery and complement government efforts in areas where public resources may be inadequate. However, the same structures that make the sector useful can also create vulnerabilities when there are weak registration, monitoring and accountability mechanisms.



This is why the EFCC’s 2026 National Terrorism Financing Risk Assessment exercise in Ibadan is particularly significant.



The Commission has raised concerns that terrorist networks are increasingly exploring crowdfunding, humanitarian interventions and informal financial channels to raise and move funds. In an environment where insecurity, kidnapping and violent extremism are already placing enormous pressure on communities, such vulnerabilities cannot be ignored.



Oyo State, therefore, must not treat the EFCC’s recommendation as merely another regulatory proposal. The state government should move decisively to establish a clear legal framework that will strengthen the registration, supervision and accountability of NPOs operating within the state.



Such legislation should not be designed to frustrate genuine humanitarian organisations. Rather, it should protect the integrity of the sector and ensure that organisations genuinely established to serve humanity are not compromised by criminal elements.



The responsibility, however, does not rest with government alone.



NPOs must also recognise that public trust comes with accountability. Organisations receiving donations, grants and humanitarian funds must maintain transparent financial records, conduct proper due diligence and ensure that their resources do not find their way into the hands of individuals or networks involved in terrorism.



The position of the Department of State Services, DSS, that registered NGOs forwarded to the Command are profiled, vetted and cleared before commencing operations is also an important layer of preventive security. But profiling and intelligence gathering must be complemented by effective legislation, financial monitoring and continuous regulatory oversight.



The Nigerian military's commitment to information sharing is equally important. Terrorism financing is not an EFCC problem alone. It is a national security challenge requiring the combined efforts of financial intelligence institutions, security agencies, government ministries, regulators, financial institutions, civil society and the organisations themselves.



The Acting Governor of Oyo State, through the Secretary to the State Government, Professor Musibau Babatunde, has already assured the EFCC that the state will do the needful to prevent NPOs from being exploited for terrorism financing. That commitment must now translate into concrete legislative and regulatory action.



Nigeria cannot afford to wait until a charity organisation, humanitarian intervention or crowdfunding platform is discovered to have financed violence before taking action.



Prevention remains cheaper, safer and more effective than responding after terrorist networks have established financial pipelines.



Security Connect therefore supports the EFCC's call for stronger regulation of the NPO sector in Oyo State and urges the state government to act swiftly. The objective should not be excessive bureaucracy, but a transparent system that protects legitimate organisations, safeguards donors and beneficiaries, strengthens public confidence and closes loopholes that criminals may exploit.



As terrorism and kidnapping continue to evolve, so must Nigeria's response.



Every legitimate financial channel must be protected. Every vulnerable sector must be strengthened. And every institution entrusted with public or humanitarian resources must be accountable.



Oyo State has been given an opportunity to strengthen one of the critical gaps in the fight against terrorism financing. The time to act is now.
