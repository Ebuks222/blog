---
title: Seven Lecturers Suspended as Kogi State Polytechnic Battles Sexual
  Harassment, Extortion
date: 2026-07-27T00:19:00.000+01:00
---
By Security Connect Global

Kogi State Polytechnic, Lokoja, has been thrown into the spotlight following the suspension of seven lecturers over allegations ranging from sexual harassment to the illegal sale of handouts and textbooks, a development that has reignited concerns about ethics, accountability, and the safety of students within tertiary institutions.

The disciplinary action, confirmed by the institution's management, comes amid growing complaints from students, particularly female students, who have expressed fears over what they describe as a resurgence of sexual harassment, extortion, and other forms of misconduct on campus.

According to sources within the institution, some lecturers allegedly demanded sexual favours or financial inducements from female students in exchange for favourable grades. The sources claimed that some students were allegedly asked to pay between ₦70,000 and ₦80,000 to secure passing marks, while others reportedly faced inappropriate sexual advances.

One of the most disturbing allegations involves a lecturer who was said to have instructed a female student to book and pay for a hotel room before he would consider passing her in his course. Although the student reportedly reported the incident to the institution's management, sources alleged that she was intimidated rather than protected, raising concerns about the willingness of victims to come forward.

The allegations have sparked renewed debate about the abuse of authority by some academics and the vulnerability of students who depend on lecturers for academic progression. Student advocacy groups have repeatedly argued that fear of victimisation often discourages victims from reporting cases of sexual harassment, allowing perpetrators to continue unchecked.

Beyond the allegations of sexual misconduct, the institution also uncovered cases involving the unauthorized sale of handouts and textbooks. The practice, which has long been criticised across several higher institutions, places additional financial pressure on students already struggling with the rising cost of education.

Confirming the disciplinary measures, the institution's Public Relations Officer, John Amos Onimisi, disclosed that six lecturers were suspended on July 22, 2026, over the illegal sale of handouts and textbooks, while one lecturer was suspended over alleged sexual harassment of female students.

He stated that the institution's disciplinary committee has commenced a thorough investigation into all the allegations, assuring stakeholders that the outcome of the investigation would be made public.

Meanwhile, some members of the institution have linked the recent developments to what they perceive as a decline in campus discipline. According to anonymous sources, enforcement of policies introduced by the previous administration—including compulsory wearing of identity cards and strict dress code regulations—has reportedly become less stringent under the current Rector, Professor Salihu Sanusi Avidime. While these claims remain unverified, they have contributed to wider discussions about institutional discipline and governance.

The suspensions have been welcomed by many students as a positive first step. However, stakeholders insist that disciplinary action alone will not address the deeper challenges unless accompanied by stronger preventive measures, transparent investigations, effective whistleblower protection, and strict sanctions against offenders.

Across Nigeria's tertiary education sector, allegations of sexual harassment have continued to generate national concern, prompting calls for comprehensive policies that protect students, encourage confidential reporting, and ensure that academic assessment is based solely on merit.

For Kogi State Polytechnic, the ongoing investigation presents an opportunity not only to determine the facts surrounding the allegations but also to reinforce public confidence in the institution's commitment to justice, accountability, and the welfare of its students.

As the disciplinary committee continues its work, students, parents, and education stakeholders will be watching closely, hoping that the outcome will send a clear message that exploitation, intimidation, and abuse of office have no place in institutions of learning.
