By Vivian Onwuasoanya



President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has commended the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) for securing 10,872 convictions and recovering ₦1.233 trillion and millions of dollars in illicit assets within the first 34 months of the current administration, calling for stronger collaboration among stakeholders in Nigeria’s anti-corruption campaign.



The President gave the commendation on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, in Abuja, at the opening ceremony of the 8th EFCC/National Judicial Institute (NJI) Workshop for Judges and Justices, themed “Investigation and Prosecution of Economic and Financial Crimes in a Digital Age.”



Represented by Vice President Kashim Shettima, Tinubu praised the Commission’s efforts in combating cryptocurrency fraud and recovering virtual assets, describing its achievements as evidence of its determination to keep pace with evolving criminal methods.



“The EFCC’s recent momentum in tackling cryptocurrency fraud and its commendable strides in recovering virtual assets is highly reassuring. These actions signal an agency that is proactive, forward-looking and resolved not to be outpaced by technological innovation,” he said.



The President disclosed that the EFCC filed 14,476 cases in court and secured 10,872 convictions within the first 34 months of the administration, representing a conviction-to-filing ratio of 75.1 per cent.



He added that the Commission recorded 1,370 convictions from 1,889 cases filed in the first half of 2026.



The EFCC also recovered ₦1.233 trillion, $684,478,457.32, £373,905.78 and €9,343,803.66 in monetary assets.



Tinubu further cited the successful prosecution and subsequent deportation of 192 foreign nationals arrested for cyberterrorism and cryptocurrency fraud as evidence of the Commission’s commitment to tackling technology-driven crimes.



He, however, stressed that the increasing sophistication of financial crimes involving blockchain technology and artificial intelligence required continuous capacity building for investigators, prosecutors and judicial officers.



“Because these modern threat typologies are constantly mutating, law enforcement and judicial authorities must engage in continuous capacity building to effectively adapt and respond to these evolving threats,” he said.



The President reaffirmed the Federal Government’s commitment to supporting judicial capacity development, describing an independent, progressive and reform-minded judiciary as indispensable to the success of the anti-corruption campaign.



He urged the judiciary to strengthen its capacity to adjudicate complex financial crime cases while respecting the constitutional independence of the courts.



Tinubu also expressed concern that major anti-corruption prosecutions could be undermined by procedural technicalities, warning that such setbacks might erode public confidence in the justice system.



He called on the EFCC and the judiciary to improve prosecutorial discipline and address procedural loopholes exploited by individuals seeking to frustrate trials.



According to him, the fight against corruption must not be regarded as the responsibility of the Executive alone but as a collective responsibility involving all arms of government.



He also acknowledged the contribution of the EFCC’s asset recovery efforts to funding social intervention programmes, including the Nigerian Education Loan Fund (NELFUND) and the Nigerian Consumer Credit Corporation (CREDICORP).



In his welcome address, EFCC Executive Chairman Ola Olukoyede attributed the Commission’s achievements to operational reforms aimed at improving investigations, prosecutions and asset recovery.



He said the EFCC was strengthening intelligence-led investigations, expanding its forensic capabilities, deepening domestic and international cooperation, and improving its understanding of emerging financial technologies.



“We do not seek simply to investigate more people; we seek to investigate more intelligently. We do not seek merely to prosecute more cases; we seek to prosecute cases that are properly investigated and capable of meeting the required standard of proof,” Olukoyede said.



He added that the Commission was committed to ensuring that asset recovery remained lawful, transparent and sustainable.



The EFCC chairman identified institutional bottlenecks, procedural delays, frivolous adjournments and frequent changes of counsel as major obstacles to the effective prosecution of complex financial crime cases.



He warned that prolonged proceedings could weaken evidence, affect witness availability and undermine public confidence in the judicial system.



Olukoyede urged judges to demonstrate integrity, courage and an unwavering commitment to the rule of law, stressing the need for rigorous and expeditious adjudication of corruption cases without compromising due process.



He said the ultimate objective was to establish a coordinated justice system in which investigators understood judicial requirements, prosecutors anticipated evidentiary challenges, forensic specialists effectively presented digital evidence, and judges possessed the knowledge required to adjudicate sophisticated financial crimes.



The Chief Justice of Nigeria, Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, also called for enhanced judicial capacity to address emerging financial crimes while upholding fair hearing, due process and judicial independence.



She noted that cryptocurrency transactions, encrypted communications, electronic records and cross-border financial activities had introduced new complexities into criminal proceedings, requiring judges to understand how digital evidence was obtained, preserved, authenticated and evaluated.



The CJN stressed that the seriousness of an allegation must not determine the outcome of a trial, insisting that the prosecution must establish its case through credible and legally admissible evidence.



She also urged judges to apply effective case management under the Administration of Criminal Justice Act, 2015, to prevent unnecessary delays without compromising the rights of parties.



Kekere-Ekun described Nigeria’s removal from the Financial Action Task Force’s Grey List on October 24, 2025, as an important milestone requiring sustained institutional commitment.



She cautioned, however, that cooperation among anti-corruption institutions must respect the constitutional independence of the judiciary and must never extend to influencing judicial decisions.



Chairman of the Senate Committee on Anti-Corruption, Senator Emmanuel Udende, identified delays in the trial and determination of economic and financial crime cases as a major challenge undermining Nigeria’s anti-corruption efforts.



He said prolonged proceedings weakened deterrence, undermined public confidence, consumed judicial resources and complicated the recovery of public funds.



Udende urged judges to strengthen case management, minimise avoidable adjournments and ensure the timely conclusion of proceedings in accordance with the law, while protecting the right of every defendant to a fair hearing.



He also called for stronger institutional cooperation and sustained capacity building to address challenges associated with digital evidence and increasingly sophisticated financial crimes.



Similarly, Chairman of the House Committee on Financial Crimes, Ginger Obinna Onwusibe, called for urgent reforms to address evidentiary challenges, trial delays and legislative gaps affecting the investigation and prosecution of financial crimes.



He pledged the National Assembly’s support for improved funding of forensic laboratories, digital investigation tools and specialised training for the EFCC.



Onwusibe also promised legislative reforms to improve the admissibility of digital evidence, facilitate the management of virtual assets and strengthen the legal framework for combating cybercrime and money laundering.



He stressed that effective justice delivery required an adequately equipped judiciary, urging judges to demonstrate firmness, fairness and impartiality in deciding financial crime cases.



The Attorney-General of the Federation and Minister of Justice, Lateef Fagbemi, SAN, emphasised that successful prosecution of financial crimes must begin with thorough investigations and the proper handling of digital evidence.

Fagbemi cautioned against measuring prosecutorial success solely by arrests or charges filed, stressing that the integrity of investigations, fairness of proceedings and sustainability of judicial outcomes were equally important.



Describing prosecutors as ministers of justice, he urged them to pursue justice through lawful means rather than seek convictions at all costs.



He also highlighted the importance of the Proceeds of Crime (Recovery and Management) Act, 2022, in providing mechanisms for tracing, preserving, recovering and managing illicit assets.



According to him, effective asset recovery must be supported by credible evidence, proper judicial processes and respect for the rights of affected persons.



The AGF called for stronger cooperation among the Ministry of Justice, EFCC, judiciary, financial intelligence institutions and other relevant agencies to confront increasingly sophisticated and borderless financial crimes.



He stressed that institutional rivalry must give way to shared responsibility, with all stakeholders committed to combating financial crimes while protecting the rights of innocent persons and upholding the rule of law.



The opening ceremony attracted members of the Bench and Bar, anti-corruption experts and other stakeholders in Nigeria’s criminal justice system.



The three-day workshop is expected to strengthen collaboration among investigators, prosecutors and judicial officers, with particular emphasis on digital evidence, effective case management and the timely adjudication of economic and financial crime cases. The workshop will end on Thursday, October 15, 2026.
