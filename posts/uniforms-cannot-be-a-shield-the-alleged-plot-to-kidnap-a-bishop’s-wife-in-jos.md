---
title: "Uniforms Cannot Be a Shield: The Alleged Plot to Kidnap a Bishop’s Wife
  in Jos"
date: 2026-08-14T19:08:00.000+01:00
---
The arrest of two members of Nigeria’s security services over an alleged plot to kidnap the wife of a bishop in Jos, Plateau State, is a disturbing reminder that criminal networks can sometimes attempt to exploit the trust and access associated with security uniforms.

According to an incident report dated August 11, 2026, a Nigerian Air Force sergeant identified as Ezra SB and a Nigerian Navy Ordinary Seaman identified as Usman KA were allegedly arrested by operatives of the Nigeria Police Force while attempting to execute the planned abduction.

The report alleges that the proposed operation was connected to an alleged ₦50 million payment made to the bishop with whom the intended victim's husband works. Investigators are said to be examining an allegation that the suspects planned to abduct the bishop's wife and demand the money as ransom.

According to the account, Sergeant Ezra was allegedly promised ₦10 million, while the naval personnel was allegedly to receive ₦5 million, if the operation succeeded.

But the alleged plot reportedly began to unravel when another naval personnel who was approached to participate rejected the offer. The individual was allegedly threatened and subsequently reported the matter to the Nigeria Police Force.

The report states that a police special team was then deployed to the location where the alleged kidnapping was expected to take place. The suspects were subsequently apprehended during the attempted operation.

A Warning to the Public

This incident, if established by investigation and the courts, goes beyond the alleged actions of two individuals. It raises a broader concern about the vulnerability of citizens when criminals attempt to use official positions, knowledge of security procedures or access to facilitate violent crimes.

For members of the public, the message is clear: never assume that a person is beyond suspicion simply because they wear a uniform or hold a position of authority.

At the same time, allegations must remain allegations until they are established through due process. The identities and roles of the suspects should not be treated as proof of guilt, and the relevant authorities must ensure a thorough, transparent investigation and prosecution where evidence supports the allegations.

The alleged intervention of the third individual is also a powerful reminder that speaking up can prevent tragedy. Reporting a planned crime before it occurs can give law enforcement the opportunity to intervene, protect potential victims and dismantle criminal plans before lives are lost.

The Bigger Lesson

Kidnapping is not merely a crime against an individual family. It creates fear across entire communities, undermines public confidence and places enormous pressure on families who may become targets.

When individuals entrusted with protecting society are accused of becoming involved in such schemes, the damage can be even greater because it threatens public confidence in the institutions they represent.

Security agencies must therefore continue to strengthen internal intelligence, background checks, accountability mechanisms and confidential channels through which personnel can report criminal conduct without fear of retaliation.

For citizens, vigilance remains essential.

A uniform should represent protection—not intimidation. A position of trust should never become a tool for exploitation. And anyone who plans to kidnap, extort or harm another person must understand that the law can reach them, regardless of the uniform they wear.

The alleged Jos kidnapping plot should therefore serve as an eye-opener to the public and a reminder to security institutions that professional integrity and accountability must remain at the heart of national security.
