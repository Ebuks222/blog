---
title: Why Former IGP Kayode Egbetokun Deserves a Fair Hearing
date: 2026-08-06T22:12:00.000+01:00
---
The inclusion of former Inspector-General of Police, Kayode Egbetokun, in a counter-affidavit filed before the Federal High Court in Abuja has sparked public debate, but legal observers have cautioned against drawing conclusions before the court has determined the facts.

The allegations arose from a counter-affidavit filed by the Nigeria Police Force and the Police Service Commission in response to a suit instituted by Citadel Oracle Concept Limited over access to the service records of Deputy Commissioner of Police Simon Asamber Lough.

Among the claims contained in the court documents is an allegation that a petition leading to criminal proceedings against Lough originated from a "fictitious Integrity Group" allegedly used during Egbetokun's tenure as Inspector-General of Police. However, the affidavit merely represents the position of one of the parties before the court and does not constitute a judicial finding.

Legal practitioners note that affidavits and counter-affidavits are intended to place the positions of litigants before the court. The allegations contained in such documents remain subject to judicial scrutiny until evidence is evaluated and a competent court reaches a decision.

The Nigerian Constitution guarantees every citizen the right to fair hearing and presumes every person innocent until proven otherwise. This constitutional safeguard applies equally to former public officials, including former Inspectors-General of Police.

During his tenure, Egbetokun led the Nigeria Police Force through several security challenges and championed reforms aimed at strengthening intelligence-led policing, improving operational efficiency, and enhancing officers' welfare. While his administration, like those before it, attracted differing opinions, supporters argue that his record should ultimately be assessed on verified facts rather than unresolved allegations.

The ongoing proceedings before the Federal High Court are expected to provide all parties with the opportunity to present evidence supporting their respective claims. Until then, the issues raised in the affidavits remain matters for judicial determination.

Legal analysts have consistently warned against trial by media, noting that public commentary should not replace the role of the courts in determining the truth of disputed allegations. They stress that the credibility of Nigeria's justice system depends on allowing legal institutions to examine evidence impartially and deliver decisions based on law.

As the case progresses, attention is expected to remain on the evidence presented before the court. Whatever the eventual outcome, the proceedings underscore the importance of due process, the rule of law, and respect for the constitutional rights of all parties involved.

Until a court delivers its judgment, the allegations against former Inspector-General Kayode Egbetokun remain unproven, and the legal process must be allowed to run its full course.
