---
title: Why No Nigerian Girl Should Miss School Because of Her Period
date: 2026-07-30T15:14:00.000+01:00
---
For millions of Nigerian girls, menstruation is more than a monthly biological process—it is a silent struggle shaped by poverty, stigma, and limited access to basic sanitary products.

Every month, countless girls are forced to miss school because they cannot afford sanitary pads. Some resort to using unsafe alternatives such as old cloths, tissue paper, or other makeshift materials, exposing them to infections, discomfort, and emotional distress. What should be a natural part of life becomes a barrier to education, confidence, and opportunity.

Studies have shown that many girls lose several days of learning every month due to period poverty. Over time, these missed school days affect academic performance, reduce self-esteem, and increase the risk of dropping out altogether.

Menstrual health is not just a personal issue—it is a matter of education, public health, gender equality, and national development. Every girl deserves the dignity of managing her period safely without sacrificing her education or dreams.

At Security Connect Global Magazine, we believe it is time to move beyond conversations and take meaningful action. We are committed to supporting initiatives that provide sanitary pads to girls in schools while raising awareness about menstrual health and hygiene.

We call on government agencies, corporate organizations, NGOs, and well-meaning Nigerians to join this mission. Together, we can ensure that no girl is denied her education, confidence, or dignity simply because of her menstrual cycle.

Menstruation is natural. Period poverty should never be.
