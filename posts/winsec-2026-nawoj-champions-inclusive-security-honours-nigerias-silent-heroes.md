---
title: "WINSEC 2026: NAWOJ Champions Inclusive Security, Honours Nigeria's
  Silent Heroes"
date: 2026-08-04T19:57:00.000+01:00
---
At a time when Nigeria continues to confront complex security challenges ranging from terrorism, banditry, kidnapping, cybercrime, communal conflicts, and other emerging threats, the need for stronger collaboration among security agencies, policymakers, civil society, and the media has never been more imperative.

Recognising this reality, the Nigeria Association of Women Journalists (NAWOJ) has taken a bold step by introducing the maiden Women in Security Summit and Awards (WINSEC 2026)—a strategic national initiative aimed at promoting inclusive security governance while celebrating individuals and institutions whose dedication continues to strengthen Nigeria's peace and security architecture.

Scheduled to hold on Wednesday, August 5, 2026, at the prestigious Transcorp Hilton Hotel, Abuja, the summit is expected to bring together an impressive gathering of security chiefs, policymakers, traditional rulers, development partners, intelligence professionals, members of the Armed Forces, the Nigeria Police Force, paramilitary agencies, civil society organisations, diplomatic representatives, media executives, and other critical stakeholders.

Speaking during a media briefing in Abuja, the National Chairperson of NAWOJ, Hajia Aishatu Ibrahim, described WINSEC 2026 as more than an awards ceremony. According to her, it is a national platform designed to encourage dialogue, strengthen partnerships, and promote innovative solutions to Nigeria's evolving security challenges.

She stressed that national security is no longer the exclusive responsibility of government and security agencies but a shared responsibility requiring active citizen participation, responsible media engagement, and strategic collaboration across all sectors.

Her position aligns with the growing recognition that sustainable security cannot be achieved through kinetic operations alone. Intelligence gathering, community policing, strategic communication, conflict prevention, and public trust remain fundamental pillars in building safer communities.

One of the defining objectives of the summit is to spotlight the indispensable role women continue to play in conflict prevention, peacebuilding, humanitarian interventions, intelligence gathering, crisis management, and community resilience. The organisers believe that expanding women's participation in security leadership and decision-making will contribute significantly to more inclusive and sustainable national security strategies.

Beyond policy discussions, the summit will also recognise distinguished individuals and organisations through the Women in Security Awards, honouring exceptional service, professionalism, integrity, courage, and commitment to national peace and public safety.

According to the organisers, the awards are designed to celebrate genuine service and inspire excellence among security personnel, journalists, humanitarian workers, community leaders, and public servants whose contributions often go unnoticed despite their impact on national development.

For the media, the summit represents another important opportunity to deepen collaboration with security institutions. As misinformation, disinformation, and cyber threats continue to shape modern security environments, journalists have an increasingly critical responsibility to promote factual reporting, strengthen public confidence, and support national cohesion through responsible journalism.

For Security Connect, whose mission has consistently focused on promoting professionalism within Nigeria's security sector while highlighting the sacrifices of officers and institutions committed to national peace, WINSEC 2026 reflects the growing understanding that effective security requires partnership, inclusiveness, and shared responsibility.

As Nigeria seeks lasting solutions to its security challenges, initiatives such as WINSEC 2026 serve as timely reminders that building a peaceful nation demands more than operational success. It requires visionary leadership, collaboration, recognition of excellence, and sustained commitment from government, security agencies, the media, traditional institutions, civil society, and every patriotic citizen.

The inaugural summit therefore represents more than an event—it signals the emergence of a national platform dedicated to strengthening security dialogue, recognising outstanding service, and inspiring collective action towards a safer, more secure, and more prosperous Nigeria.
