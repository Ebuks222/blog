---
title: Zone 5 Police Trains Officers on Gender-Based Violence Response
date: 2026-10-17T19:24:00.000+01:00
---
By Vivian Onwuasoanya



The Nigeria Police Force, Zone 5 Command Headquarters, Benin City, has organised a two-day capacity-building programme for police officers on gender-based violence (GBV) response, aimed at strengthening personnel’s capacity to handle cases professionally, sensitively and effectively.



The training, held on October 14, 2026, was organised by the Edo State Administration of Criminal Justice Monitoring Committee (ACJMC) in collaboration with the Assistant Inspector-General of Police, Zone 5 Command Headquarters, with support from the International Organization for Migration (IOM).



The programme brought together officers from the Zone 5 Headquarters and the Edo and Delta State Police Commands to enhance their understanding of gender-based violence, improve investigative and response mechanisms, and promote victim-centred policing.



The training featured theoretical and practical sessions facilitated by Mrs Esosa Osula, Esq., Secretary of the Edo State Administration of Criminal Justice Monitoring Committee, alongside resource persons from the Edo State Gender-Based Violence Centre and the Ministry of Women Affairs.



Delivering the opening remarks, the Deputy Commissioner of Police in charge of the Zonal Criminal Investigation Department, DCP Gabriel Dibie, PhD, psc, welcomed the participants and urged them to remain focused and actively engage in the sessions to maximise the benefits of the training.



Key areas covered included understanding gender norms and gender-based violence, the legal and policy frameworks for GBV prevention and response, policing with empathy, and the responsibilities of police officers in handling gender-based violence cases.



Participants also received practical demonstrations on basic life support and cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), delivered by paramedic Mr Blessed Omokhomen of SAVAN/UBTH. The hands-on session equipped officers with essential knowledge of emergency response and life-saving techniques.



The training was described as insightful and beneficial, with participants gaining practical knowledge expected to improve their preparedness and professionalism in responding to gender-based violence incidents.



The Assistant Inspector-General of Police in charge of Zone 5 Headquarters, Benin City, AIG Monday Agbonika,  commended the ACJMC and IOM for supporting the initiative.



He noted that the programme reflected the commitment of the Nigeria Police Force and its partners to strengthening institutional collaboration and sustaining joint efforts to combat gender-based violence.



Speaking on behalf of the AIG and the participants, the Deputy Commissioner of Police in charge of Operations, DCP Akpama Okoi,  expressed appreciation to the organisers and assured them that the knowledge and practical skills acquired would be applied in the discharge of police duties whenever necessary.



The Zone 5 Command reaffirmed its appreciation to the ACJMC and IOM for their continued cooperation, expressing its commitment to further collaborative initiatives that promote public safety, professional policing and effective responses to gender-based violence.



The initiative underscores the importance of continuous capacity building, inter-agency partnerships and a victim-centred approach in ensuring that survivors of gender-based violence receive appropriate, sensitive and effective responses from law enforcement officers.



SP Doghudje A. Johnson
