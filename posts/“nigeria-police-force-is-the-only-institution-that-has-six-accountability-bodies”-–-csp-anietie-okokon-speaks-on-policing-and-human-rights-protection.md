---
title: “Nigeria Police Force Is the Only Institution That Has Six Accountability
  Bodies” – CSP Anietie Okokon Speaks on Policing and Human Rights Protection
date: 2026-08-07T09:09:00.000+01:00
---
The Nigeria Police Force has reaffirmed its commitment to transparency, accountability, and the protection of human rights, with senior police officer CSP Anietie Okokon stating that the Force remains one of the most scrutinised public institutions in the country.

Speaking during an interview on Morning Express, CSP Okokon noted that the Nigeria Police Force operates under no fewer than six accountability mechanisms, making it the only institution in Nigeria with such an extensive oversight structure.

According to him, these accountability bodies are designed to ensure that police officers carry out their constitutional responsibilities professionally, ethically, and in full compliance with the rule of law while protecting the rights of citizens.

He explained that policing in a democratic society goes beyond enforcing the law; it also involves respecting human dignity, safeguarding fundamental rights, and maintaining public confidence through transparency and accountability.

CSP Okokon stressed that every police officer is expected to uphold the highest standards of integrity and professionalism, adding that misconduct by officers is neither encouraged nor tolerated by the leadership of the Force. He noted that established disciplinary and complaint mechanisms exist to investigate allegations of misconduct and ensure that officers found wanting are sanctioned in accordance with the law.

He further observed that over the years, the Nigeria Police Force has continued to strengthen its internal accountability systems while embracing reforms aimed at improving public trust. According to him, these reforms include enhanced training on human rights, ethics, community policing, and the appropriate use of force during law enforcement operations.

The police spokesman also highlighted the role of external oversight institutions and the judiciary in ensuring that the actions of police personnel remain subject to legal and constitutional scrutiny. This multi-layered accountability framework, he said, reflects the Force's commitment to operating within the confines of the law and respecting democratic principles.

Speaking on human rights protection, CSP Okokon maintained that the Inspector-General of Police has consistently directed officers across the country to discharge their duties with civility, professionalism, and respect for the rights of all Nigerians. He noted that officers are continually reminded that public confidence remains one of the strongest pillars of effective policing.

He urged members of the public to report any act of misconduct or abuse of office by police personnel through the appropriate complaint channels, assuring that genuine complaints receive prompt attention and investigation.

According to him, building a safer society requires collaboration between the police and the communities they serve. He encouraged citizens to continue supporting the Nigeria Police Force by providing credible information that would assist in crime prevention and investigation while holding officers accountable through lawful channels.

CSP Okokon's remarks come at a time when policing reforms and human rights protection continue to occupy a central place in national conversations on security and justice. His comments reinforce the Nigeria Police Force's position that accountability, professionalism, and respect for human rights remain essential to effective and modern policing.
